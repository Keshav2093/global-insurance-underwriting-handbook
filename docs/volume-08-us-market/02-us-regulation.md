# 8.2 US Regulation

## Overview

The **US insurance regulatory system** is state-based. Each state regulates the business of insurance within its borders — insurer solvency, market conduct, rates, forms, producer licensing, and consumer protection. The **NAIC** provides coordination, while a limited **federal role** has developed through the Federal Insurance Office (FIO) and specialized statutes (e.g., Dodd-Frank, TRIA).

## The Constitutional Basis

- The **McCarran-Ferguson Act (1945)** grants states the authority to regulate the "business of insurance" without federal preemption.
- States define insurance, license insurers and producers, and regulate rates and forms.
- **Federal law** applies to insurance only where a specific statute directs (e.g., antitrust, RICO, employment).

## Key Regulators

| Regulator | Role |
|---|---|
| **State insurance department** | Solvency, market conduct, licensing, consumer protection |
| **Nationwide association (NAIC)** | Model laws, financial standards, accreditation, interstate coordination |
| **Federal Insurance Office (FIO)** | Federal monitoring, international agreements (Treasury) |
| **Federal Reserve / FSOC** | Insurance companies deemed systemically important; thrift holding companies |
| **Department of Labor** | ERISA plans; fiduciary and claims standards |
| **SEC** | Variable life/annuity and securities-linked products |

## State Regulatory Functions

| Function | Description |
|---|---|
| **Licensing/admission** | Certificate of authority to transact business |
| **Financial regulation** | Capital, reserves, RBC, examinations |
| **Rate regulation** | Filing, approval, or use-and-file |
| **Form regulation** | Policy form and endorsement filings |
| **Market conduct** | Marketing, underwriting, claims, complaints |
| **Producer licensing** | Agents, brokers, adjusters, MGAs |
| **Consumer protection** | Complaints, unfair trade practices, market conduct exams |

## NAIC Model Laws and Accreditation

The **NAIC** develops model laws that states may adopt:

| Model | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Risk-Based Capital (RBC)** | Capital requirements scaled to risk |
| **Financial examination** | Uniform examination standards |
| **Market conduct annual statement** | Uniform market conduct reporting |
| **Unfair Claims Settlement Practices** | Fair claim handling standards |
| **Privacy (GLBA)** | Consumer financial privacy protections |
| **Insurance Holding Company** | Group supervision and solvency |

States that adopt key models are **accredited** — accreditation is required for regulatory cooperation.

## Rate and Form Regulation

| System | Description | States |
|---|---|---|
| **Prior approval** | Rates/forms in effect only after approval | Many personal lines states |
| **Use-and-file** | Rates used; filed after use | Some commercial states |
| **File-and-use** | Filed before/with use; effective then | Liberal states |
| **Open competition** | No formal filing; market discipline | Larger commercial states |
| **Flex rating** | Rate changes within band without filing | A few states |

See [8.3 Forms, Rates & Rules](03-forms-rates-rules.md).

## Market Conduct Regulation

| Area | Key Requirements |
|---|---|
| **Underwriting** | No unfair discrimination (protected classes) |
| **Rates** | Not excessive, inadequate, or unfairly discriminatory |
| **Claims** | Fair claims practice acts; prompt, timely handling |
| **Advertising** | Truthful; no misleading statements |
| **Privacy** | GLBA financial privacy rules |
| **Complaints** | Respond to state regulator inquiries |

States conduct **market conduct examinations** — targeted or full-scope reviews of an insurer's business practices.

## The Federal Role

| Statute | Scope |
|---|---|
| **McCarran-Ferguson** | State authority; limited antitrust exemption |
| **Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA)** | Federal backstop for certified terrorism losses |
| **Dodd-Frank** | FIO, FSOC, federal oversight of systemic risk |
| **Flood Insurance (NFIP)** | Federally administered flood program |
| **Surplus lines (NRRA)** | Surplus lines licensing reform — single home state |
| **ERISA** | Employee benefit plan regulation |

## Insurance Guaranty Funds

- Each state has a **guaranty fund** for insolvent insurers.
- Funds pay covered claims up to statutory limits.
- Funded by assessments on insurers based on premium.
- Cover **admitted insurers**; surplus lines have separate mechanisms.

## Consumer Protection

| Protection | Practice |
|---|---|
| **Free look** | Life/health cancellation periods |
| **Cancellation rules** | Notice periods and reasons |
| **Renewal/non-renewal notice** | Statutory notice requirements |
| **Fraud bureau** | State fraud investigation units |
| **Complaint handling** | State complaint process and records |

## Regulatory Trends

| Trend | Impact |
|---|---|
| **NAIC modernization** | Data collection, cyber, climate risk |
| **Rate transparency** | Public rate filing data |
| **Consumer duty** | Conduct-of-business standards |
| **Climate risk** | Disclosure and solvency scrutiny |
| **Technology** | Regulatory sandboxes, AI guidance |

## Summary

- The US system is **state-based**, coordinated by the **NAIC**.
- States regulate **solvency, rates, forms, market conduct, and licensing**.
- The **federal role** is limited but growing (FIO, TRIA, systemic risk).
- **Guaranty funds** protect policyholders from insolvency.
- Insurers must navigate **state-specific filing and conduct rules**.
