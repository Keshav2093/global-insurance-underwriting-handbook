# 8.1 US Market Structure

## Overview

The **US insurance market** is the world's largest, characterized by state-based regulation, a wide range of distribution channels, and a deep reinsurance market. It is a **property/casualty and life/health** market served by thousands of insurers, spanning personal, commercial, and specialty lines.

## Market Segments

| Segment | Description |
|---|---|
| **Personal lines** | Auto, homeowners, renters, umbrella |
| **Commercial lines** | Property, casualty, auto, workers comp, packages |
| **Specialty lines** | Marine, aviation, energy, cyber, D&O, E&O |
| **Life & annuity** | Life insurance, annuities, retirement |
| **Health** | Medical, disability, long-term care |
| **Reinsurance** | Domestic and international capacity |

## Market Participants

| Participant | Role |
|---|---|
| **Insurers** | Primary risk bearers (stock, mutual, reciprocal) |
| **Reinsurers** | Capacity and risk transfer above primary limits |
| **Producers** | Independent agents, captive agents, brokers |
| **MGAs/MGUs** | Delegated underwriting and policy issuance |
| **TPAs** | Claims and administration services |
| **Advisory organizations** | ISO, NCCI, AAIS — forms and data |
| **Regulators** | State insurance departments and NAIC |

## Admitted vs. Non-Admitted

| Market | Description |
|---|---|
| **Admitted** | Licensed in each state; regulated rates/forms; guaranty fund protection |
| **Non-admitted (surplus lines)** | Not licensed; flexible terms; no guaranty fund; surplus lines broker required |

## Distribution

| Channel | Description |
|---|---|
| **Independent agents** | Represent multiple insurers |
| **Captive agents** | Represent one insurer |
| **Brokers** | Represent the insured |
| **Direct** | Internet, telephone, captive |
| **MGAs** | Delegated underwriting authority |
| **Banks** | Bancassurance partnerships |

## Reinsurance Market

| Type | Role |
|---|---|
| **Treaty** | Automatic cession of portfolios |
| **Facultative** | Individual risk placement |
| **Proportional** | Pro-rata sharing of premium and losses |
| **Excess of loss** | Cover above a retention |

The US is served by global reinsurers (Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re) and domestic specialists.

## Characteristics

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **State-based regulation** | 50+ jurisdictions with separate rules |
| **Rate regulation** | Prior approval, file-and-use, open competition |
| **Guaranty funds** | State insolvency protection for policyholders |
| **Tort environment** | Litigious market; social inflation trends |
| **Catastrophe exposure** | Hurricanes, wildfire, earthquake, convective storms |
| **Technology adoption** | Insurtech, telematics, AI, usage-based insurance |

## US Market Cycle

| Phase | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| **Hard market** | Capacity tight; rates rise; terms restrict |
| **Soft market** | Capacity abundant; rates fall; terms broaden |
| **Transition** | Event-driven inflection (e.g., catastrophes, investment yields) |

## Summary

- The US market is the **world's largest insurance market**.
- **State regulation** and **admitted/non-admitted** structure define the market.
- **Distribution** spans agents, brokers, direct, and MGAs.
- **Reinsurance** provides capacity and stability.
- The market is **cyclical**, influenced by capacity, losses, and investments.
