# 4.7 Portfolio Management

## Overview

Portfolio management is the discipline of shaping and monitoring the **aggregate book of business**. Individual risk selection alone is not enough: losses are driven not only by how many bad risks are written, but by **how risks correlate and concentrate**.

The underwriting portfolio must be managed to meet:

- **Profitability targets** — combined ratio, return on capital.
- **Capital constraints** — solvency capital, reinsurance attachment.
- **Appetite** — lines, territories, classes, limits.
- **Diversification** — across causes of loss, geography, and accounts.

## Key Portfolio Concepts

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Aggregation** | Total exposure from many policies that can be lost in one event |
| **Concentration** | Heavy dependence on a class, territory, account, or peril |
| **Correlation** | Losses that rise together (wind and flood; cyber and cloud) |
| **Diversification** | Spreading risk across uncorrelated exposures |
| **Catastrophe limit (CAT)** | Maximum modeled loss the company will retain (net of reinsurance) |
| **Natural catastrophe model** | Probabilistic model of hurricanes, earthquakes, flood, etc. |
| **Worst-case / PML** | Probable maximum loss at various return periods |

## Monitoring the Portfolio

| Metric | Description |
|---|---|
| **Exposure by territory** | Sum insured by zone (flood, wind, wildfire, earthquake) |
| **Limit concentration** | Largest accounts as % of surplus |
| **Class concentration** | % premium from a single class |
| **Geographic concentration** | % exposure in one territory |
| **Reinsurance ceded** | % of limit ceded; retention levels |
| **Loss ratio trends** | Actual vs. expected by segment |
| **Rate adequacy** | Price monitoring vs. benchmark |
| **New business mix** | Growth segments and their profitability |

## Aggregation and CAT Management

### The PML Process

For CAT perils:

1. **Exposure accumulation** — sum insured by peril zone.
2. **Probabilistic modeling** — run event sets (hurricane, earthquake, flood).
3. **Determine PML** — worst-case loss at return periods (e.g., 1-in-100, 1-in-250).
4. **Compare to appetite** — net of reinsurance vs. capital.
5. **Act** — limit new business, buy reinsurance, adjust retentions.

### Aggregation Controls

| Control | Example |
|---|---|
| **Per-risk limits** | Max sum insured per location |
| **Per-event limits** | Max exposure in a flood/wind zone |
| **Per-account limits** | Cap on any one insured |
| **Zone blocks** | Stop writing when a zone capacity is reached |
| **Reinsurance program** | Cat XL, per-risk XL, proportional cover |

## Concentration Analysis

| Concentration | Risk |
|---|---|
| **Single account** | Loss of a major account undermines profit |
| **Single class** | Systemic claims (e.g., all restaurants) |
| **Single territory** | One CAT event hits the whole book |
| **Single peril** | Wind-only book; flood-only book |
| **Single producer** | Broker distribution risk |
| **Single reinsurer** | Counterparty credit risk |

Concentration limits are set in **underwriting guidelines** and enforced by the portfolio manager.

## Planning and Appetite

### Setting Appetite

- **Risk tolerance** — what the company will accept (return period, PML).
- **Capital allocation** — how much capital each line consumes.
- **Return targets** — required ROE; pricing floor.
- **Line capacity** — limits per risk, per event, per account.

### The Business Plan

The underwriting plan translates strategy into numbers:

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| **Premium plan** | New business, renewal retention, rate change |
| **Mix plan** | By line, class, territory |
| **Capacity plan** | Limits, aggregate exposures |
| **Reinsurance plan** | Structure, retention, cost |
| **Pricing plan** | Rate levels, credits, debits |

The plan is monitored **monthly/quarterly** against actual results; variances trigger action.

## Cycle Management

The insurance cycle drives portfolio decisions:

| Phase | Underwriting Response |
|---|---|
| **Soft market** | Rates fall; consider reducing exposure; maintain discipline; avoid writing at inadequate rates |
| **Hard market** | Rates rise; capacity returns; re-underwrite; write to improved terms |
| **Transition** | Watch loss trends; adjust appetite before the cycle turns |

Cycle discipline means **not mistaking volume for profit** in the soft phase and **not losing relationships** in the hard phase.

## Reinsurance and Portfolio Protection

Reinsurance protects the portfolio:

| Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Proportional (quota share, surplus)** | Share risk and premium with a reinsurer |
| **Excess of loss (per risk)** | Protect individual large losses |
| **Catastrophe XL** | Protect against single-event aggregation |
| **Stop loss** | Protect the loss ratio of the whole portfolio |
| **Facultative** | Specific risk-by-risk protection |

The underwriter must understand the **net position** — what the company retains after reinsurance — and write within it.

## Emerging Risks and the Portfolio

| Trend | Portfolio Impact |
|---|---|
| **Climate change** | Rising flood/wildfire frequency; model uncertainty |
| **Cyber aggregation** | Systemic risk through common cloud providers |
| **Social inflation** | Liability severity rising; litigation funding |
| **Litigation funding** | More claims; larger settlements |
| **Supply chain** | Contingent BI concentration |
| **Pandemic** | Business interruption correlation |

Portfolio managers incorporate **forward-looking risk** into appetites and models.

## Summary

- Portfolio management shapes the **aggregate risk** of the book.
- Monitoring uses **exposure, concentration, loss ratio, and rate adequacy** metrics.
- **CAT limits, PML, and aggregation controls** prevent catastrophe concentration.
- **The business plan and appetite** translate strategy into limits.
- **Cycle discipline** balances growth and profit.
- **Reinsurance** manages the net retained position.
- **Emerging risks** (climate, cyber, social inflation) are portfolio-level threats.
