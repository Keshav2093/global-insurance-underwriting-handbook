# 4.4 Risk Assessment & COPE

## Overview

**Risk assessment** is the underwriter's analysis of the probability and severity of loss for a given risk. It blends:

- **Physical hazard** — the tangible features that affect loss (construction, occupancy, condition).
- **Moral hazard** — the behavioral risk that the insured will cause or exaggerate loss.
- **Morale hazard** — indifference or carelessness because insurance exists.
- **Legal/contractual exposure** — liability, contractual obligations.
- **Financial exposure** — the size and adequacy of limits and values.

For property risks the classic framework is **COPE**: Construction, Occupancy, Protection, Exposure.

## Hazard Analysis

### Physical Hazard

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Construction** | Frame, masonry, fire-resistive; roof; building systems |
| **Occupancy** | Business type, processes, storage, people |
| **Housekeeping** | Clutter, blocked exits, waste handling |
| **Maintenance** | Roof, electrical, plumbing, HVAC condition |
| **Protection** | Sprinklers, alarms, extinguishers, hydrants |
| **Exposure** | Adjacent buildings, wildfire, flood, crime |
| **Contrast** | Vehicle type and safety features |
| **Condition** | Age, wear, prior damage, deferred maintenance |

### Moral Hazard

Moral hazard is the risk of **intentional or exaggerated loss**:

| Signal | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Financial distress** | Debts, bankruptcy, declining business |
| **Overinsurance** | Sum insured far above value |
| **Claims history** | Frequency out of proportion; prior fraud |
| **Behavioral patterns** | Recent large purchases, policy changes before loss |
| **Vague information** | Incomplete, inconsistent, or evasive responses |

### Morale Hazard

Morale hazard is **carelessness** enabled by insurance:

- Lack of basic loss prevention (no locks, no extinguishers).
- Poor housekeeping and maintenance.
- Failure to follow loss control recommendations.
- Indifference to claims frequency (small claims repeatedly).

## COPE — The Property Risk Framework

### Construction

| Type | Fire Characteristics |
|---|---|
| **Frame (wood)** | Highest combustibility; rapid fire spread |
| **Joisted masonry** | Exterior masonry, combustible interior; good but less than fire-resistive |
| **Masonry non-combustible** | Exterior and structure non-combustible; lower risk |
| **Fire-resistive** | Protected steel/concrete; best fire performance |
| **Metal** | Fast heat conduction; not fire rated itself |

Other construction factors:

- **Roof** — materials (metal, asphalt, wood shake), condition, age.
- **Occupancy load** — height, area, openings.
- **Building systems** — electrical, heating, HVAC; age and condition.
- **Maintenance** — evidence of deferred maintenance.

### Occupancy

| Consideration | Underwriting Questions |
|---|---|
| **Hazard** | What operations, materials, processes? |
| **Density** | People load; storage concentration |
| **Fire load** | Combustible inventory and packaging |
| **Special hazards** | Cooking, chemicals, welding, dust, flammable liquids |
| **Vehicles on premises** | Parking, loading, repair operations |
| **Tenants** | Multiple occupancies; tenant operations |
| **Hours** | Operations while unoccupied (night risk) |

### Protection

**Public protection:**

- Fire department distance, staffing, water supply.
- Response time and capability.

**Private protection:**

| System | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Automatic sprinklers** | The single most effective fire control |
| **Fire alarms** | Early detection; monitored vs. local |
| **Standpipes/hydrants** | Water supply for firefighting |
| **Extinguishers** | First-aid firefighting |
| **Special systems** | Kitchen hood suppression, alarm verification |
| **Security systems** | Burglar alarms, video, doors |

Protection **conditions** are often policy warranties — if the insured disconnects protection, coverage may be voided at the loss.

### Exposure

**External exposures:**

- **Adjacent properties** — proximity, construction, occupancy of neighbors.
- **Wildfire** — Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) zones.
- **Flood** — FEMA flood zones; floodways; storm surge.
- **Wind** — hurricane, tornado zones.
- **Earthquake** — seismic zones.
- **Crime** — burglary/theft territory.
- **Other** — airports, railroads, chemical plants, dense commercial.

The underwriter assesses the **neighborhood effect**: what is around the risk determines frequency and severity of certain losses.

## Underwriting the Liability Risk

Liability is assessed differently than property:

| Exposure | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Premises** | Condition, visitors, hazards (slip/trip), security |
| **Operations** | Processes off/on site, supervision, subcontracting |
| **Products** | Nature, distribution, hazard, recall history |
| **Completed operations** | Duration of exposure, contract terms |
| **Contracts** | Indemnity, additional insured, waiver of subrogation |
| **Litigation climate** | Territory, legal environment, claim trends |

## Financial Exposure Assessment

| Question | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Is the limit adequate for the worst credible loss? | Determine whether the requested limit is sensible |
| Are values adequate (coinsurance)? | Validate premium and coverage adequacy |
| Can the insured sustain a deductible/SIR? | Financial retentions must be credible |
| Is the premium affordable and payment reliable? | Lapse and non-payment risk |
| Are there moral hazard signals in the financials? | Distress → fraud/lapse risk |

## Qualitative Assessment

Underwriters also weigh **qualitative** factors:

- **Management quality** — experience, safety culture, responsiveness.
- **Housekeeping** — physical reflection of management attitude.
- **Information quality** — a clean, complete submission signals professionalism.
- **Broker/agent relationship** — track record and reliability.
- **Risk improvement receptiveness** — willingness to act on recommendations.

## The Risk Assessment Output

The assessment should conclude:

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| **Risk classification** | Acceptable / borderline / unacceptable |
| **Exposure analysis** | Worst credible loss, frequency drivers |
| **Loss control needs** | Conditions, warranties, inspections |
| **Recommended terms** | Limits, deductibles, exclusions, endorsements |
| **Pricing indication** | Rate basis, loadings, credits |
| **Documentation** | Basis of the decision recorded |

## Common Assessment Errors

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|
| Overweighting recent large loss | Analyze trend and cause, not just a single event |
| Ignoring small claim frequency | Recognize behavior signals |
| Anchoring on the submitted values | Independently estimate values |
| Under-estimating CAT exposure | Model and check aggregates |
| Relying on one information source | Triangulate application, loss runs, inspection |
| Confusing moral and morale hazard | Treat causes separately in response |

## Summary

- Risk assessment blends **physical, moral, morale, legal, and financial** analysis.
- **COPE** (construction, occupancy, protection, exposure) is the framework for property.
- **Liability and financial exposure** require separate analytical lenses.
- **Qualitative factors** (management, housekeeping, information quality) distinguish good risks from bad.
- The output is a documented **classification, terms, and pricing** decision.
