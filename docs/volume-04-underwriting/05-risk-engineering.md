# 4.5 Risk Engineering & Loss Control

## Overview

**Risk engineering** (also called **loss control** or **risk improvement**) is the technical discipline that identifies and mitigates the physical and operational causes of loss. It supports underwriting by:

- **Validating** the information in the submission.
- **Identifying hazards** not visible from paper.
- **Recommending controls** that reduce frequency and severity.
- **Supporting price** — better protected risks deserve better terms.
- **Meeting policy conditions** — inspections required by guidelines or reinsurance.

## The Risk Engineering Process

```
Screening → Survey/Inspection → Analysis → Report → Follow-up
```

| Step | Activity |
|---|---|
| **Screening** | Decide if the account requires a survey (value, occupancy, hazard) |
| **Survey** | On-site inspection by engineer or trained surveyor |
| **Analysis** | Evaluate COPE, exposures, controls, management |
| **Report** | Document findings, recommendations, PIR scores |
| **Follow-up** | Track recommendation implementation |

## Types of Surveys

| Survey Type | Scope |
|---|---|
| **Photo survey** | Exterior and basic interior condition; no engineering detail |
| **Drive-by** | Neighborhood, construction, exterior hazards |
| **Basic property survey** | COPE fields, protection, housekeeping |
| **Full engineering survey** | Detailed systems, fire protection, occupancy, process hazards |
| **Boiler & machinery** | Pressure vessels, electrical, mechanical systems |
| **Liability survey** | Premises, operations, products exposure |
| **Environmental assessment** | Phase I/II site assessments |

## Key Engineering Considerations

### Fire Protection Engineering

The core of property loss control:

| System | Engineering Focus |
|---|---|
| **Sprinkler systems** | Design standard, water supply, coverage density, maintenance |
| **Fire pumps** | Capacity, reliability, testing |
| **Water supply** | Hydrant flow, storage, reliability |
| **Fire alarms** | Detection, monitoring, response |
| **Special suppression** | Kitchen hoods, foam, gas systems |
| **Fire doors** | Compartmentation, closures |
| **Egress** | Exit paths, signage, capacity |

### Occupancy and Process Hazards

- Wet processes, machining, heat treating.
- Flammable and combustible liquid storage.
- Dust collection and housekeeping (combustible dust).
- Welding and hot work programs.
- Storage height and racking stability.
- Material handling and conveyors.

### Natural Hazards

- **Wind** — roof systems, glazing, tie-downs.
- **Earthquake** — structural bracing, anchorage.
- **Flood** — elevation, barriers, wet location storage.
- **Wildfire** — defensible space, combustible vegetation.

### Crime and Security

- Perimeter security (fencing, lighting, locks).
- Alarm systems, video surveillance.
- Cash handling and safe procedures.
- Employee screening (pre-employment checks).

### Liability Engineering

- Premises maintenance (walkways, parking, stairs).
- Slip/trip hazards and housekeeping.
- Product quality control.
- Contractor qualification and supervision.
- Signage and warnings.

## The Engineering Report

A professional report includes:

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Executive summary** | Overall risk quality, key findings |
| **Risk description** | Construction, occupancy, protection, exposure |
| **Loss history analysis** | Claims drivers and trends |
| **Evaluations** | Systems, processes, controls |
| **Recommendations** | Prioritized improvements |
| **PIR (Protection Impairment Rating)** | Where applicable |

### The PIR Score

The **Protection Impairment Rating (PIR)** qualifies the reliability of fire protection:

| PIR | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0–10** | Superb protection availability and reliability |
| **11–20** | Good protection with minor impairments |
| **21–30** | Fair; significant impairments or reliance on public fire |
| **31–50** | Poor; substantial protection deficiencies |
| **Over 50** | Unacceptable; requires major improvement |

PIR affects pricing and acceptance. A high PIR may require **rectification** before binding or a **premium loading**.

## Recommendations and Penalties

| Recommendation Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Required before binding** | Install sprinkler system; correct electrical hazard |
| **Required during term** | Fix fire door closure; implement hot work permit |
| **Recommended** | Improve housekeeping; add extinguishers |
| **Advisory** | Consider water leak detection; business continuity |

**Warranties and conditions** formalize required improvements. Failure to comply can:

- Void coverage (warranty breach).
- Increase deductible (PIR penalty).
- Trigger cancellation or non-renewal.

## Risk Engineering Data and Analytics

Modern risk engineering uses data:

| Data Source | Use |
|---|---|
| **Sensor/IoT data** | Water leaks, temperature, equipment vibration |
| **Aerial imagery** | Roof condition, wildfire fuel, exposure |
| **Geospatial analytics** | Flood, wind, wildfire scoring |
| **Claims analytics** | Cause-of-loss patterns, cost drivers |
| **Inspection data** | Benchmarking by class and account |

Underwriters integrate engineering findings with pricing models and portfolio analytics.

## Engineering vs. Underwriting

| Issue | Handling |
|---|---|
| **Major deficiency** | Requirement, warranty, or decline |
| **Minor deficiency** | Recommendation; price consideration |
| **Excellent protection** | Credit; broaden terms |
| **Impairment of protection** | PIR penalty; require rectification |
| **Management resistance** | Underwrite the attitude, not just the building |

## Summary

- Risk engineering **validates the risk** and identifies improvement opportunities.
- **Surveys** range from photos to full engineering assessments.
- **Fire protection reliability (PIR)** and protection conditions drive terms.
- **Recommendations** become warranties when required.
- **Data and analytics** increasingly augment the engineer's judgment.
