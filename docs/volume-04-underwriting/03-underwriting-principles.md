# 4.3 Underwriting Principles

## Overview

Underwriting rests on a set of **principles** that guide consistent, profitable risk selection. These principles are the intellectual framework beneath the process described in [4.1](01-underwriting-process.md) and the data described in [4.2](02-information-sources.md).

## The Core Underwriting Principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Selection** | Choose risks that fit the insurer's appetite; decline or modify those that do not |
| **Classification** | Group similar risks so that pricing reflects differences in expected loss |
| **Anti-selection control** | Prevent adverse selection by those who know their risk is worse than average |
| **Pricing discipline** | Charge what the risk costs, regardless of market pressure |
| **Adequacy and margins** | Premium must cover expected losses, expenses, capital, and profit |
| **Portfolio balance** | Diversify; avoid dangerous concentrations |
| **Documentation** | Record the basis of each decision |
| **Fairness and legality** | Apply consistent, non-discriminatory standards |

## Selection and Adverse Selection

**Adverse selection** occurs when those most likely to claim are most motivated to buy insurance. Without underwriting control:

- The insured knows more about their risk than the insurer.
- High-risk individuals buy more coverage; low-risk individuals buy less or none.
- The pool deteriorates and premiums must rise, driving more low-risks away (the "death spiral").

### Underwriting Responses to Adverse Selection

| Response | Example |
|---|---|
| **Screening** | Applications, questionnaires, medical underwriting |
| **Third-party data** | MVR, CLUE, MIB, credit scores |
| **Inspection** | Property surveys, medical exams |
| **Classification and rating** | Higher rates for higher risk groups |
| **Restrictions** | Limits, exclusions, deductibles |
| **Participation requirements** | Group insurance minimum enrollment |
| **Waiting periods / moratoriums** | Pre-existing condition clauses |

## Classification

Classification groups risks by factors that **predict loss**, such as:

- Age, gender, territory.
- Construction, occupancy, protection, exposure.
- Class code and occupation.
- Vehicle type and usage.
- Health and lifestyle.
- Claims experience.

### Classification Criteria

| Criterion | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Statistical credibility** | Factor is supported by data |
| **Causal relevance** | Factor relates to expected loss |
| **Practicality** | Easy to collect, objective, verifiable |
| **Fairness** | Not unfairly discriminatory or prohibited |

### Prohibited Classification

- Race, religion, national origin, color.
- Gender (in some lines and jurisdictions, e.g., EU gender directive).
- Genetic information (US GINA).
- Marital status (in some jurisdictions).
- Medical history (in some cases, e.g., ACA individual health plans).

The underwriter must know **what factors are lawful** in each line and jurisdiction.

## Pricing Discipline

Pricing discipline means:

- **Price the risk, not the competition** — do not sacrifice adequacy to win business.
- **Use the pricing tools** — manual rates, experience, schedule rating, modeling.
- **Understand the cost structure** — loss costs, LAE, acquisition, fixed expenses, capital.
- **Set and respect minimum premiums** — small policies have fixed costs.
- **Review adequacy continuously** — rate levels, loss trends, model outputs.

Pricing discipline is undermined by:

- Soft-market competition (rate cutting to gain share).
- Commission pressure.
- Misleading loss experience (immature data).
- Ignoring **IBNR** (incurred but not reported).
- Underestimating **tail risk** (long-tail lines).

## Risk Appetite

**Risk appetite** is the insurer's stated willingness to accept risk, defined by:

| Dimension | Example |
|---|---|
| **Lines and classes** | What we write |
| **Territories** | Where we write |
| **Limits** | How much per risk |
| **Aggregate exposure** | Total CAT limit |
| **Acceptance criteria** | Eligibility rules, minimum standards |
| **Reinsurance** | Net retained exposure |

Appetite is documented in **underwriting guidelines** and enforced through **authority levels** and **referral rules**.

## Portfolio Balance

Individual risk selection is not enough — the **portfolio** must be managed:

- **Concentration** — avoid excess exposure in one class, territory, or account.
- **Correlation** — losses that occur together (wind + flood in the same event).
- **Aggregation** — sum of individual limits across a CAT zone.
- **Cycle management** — write at adequate rates in soft markets; protect capital.

Portfolio management is covered in detail in [4.7](07-portfolio-management.md).

## Moral Hazard and Fraud

**Moral hazard** is the risk that the insured will cause or exaggerate a loss because they benefit from it.

| Signal | Response |
|---|---|
| Overinsurance (sum >> value) | Cap limits; verify values |
| Recent large purchases + claims | Investigate; document |
| Financial distress | Review credit; payables/underinsurance |
| Claims history out of proportion | Verify loss runs; inspection |
| Recent policy changes before a loss | Investigate date-of-loss coverage |
| Vague or inconsistent information | Request specifics; verify independently |

Fraud control complements underwriting: **SIU** referrals, data analytics, and claims scrutiny.

## Fairness, Non-Discrimination, and Compliance

- **Unfair discrimination** — charging materially different rates for the same risk without data support, or treating equals unequally.
- **Fair discrimination** — charging actuarially justified differences.
- **Regulated lines** — rates and forms must be filed and approved (US) or satisfy fair value (UK FCA).
- **Adverse action notices** — consumer-report-based decisions trigger disclosure rules.

The underwriter's duty is to classify **on risk factors with evidence**, and to document the support.

## Judgment and Its Limits

Underwriting involves **professional judgment**, but discipline requires:

- **Structured frameworks** — COPE, exposure analysis, decision checklists.
- **Calibration** — compare your decisions to outcomes; learn from claims.
- **Peer review** — referrals, audits, committee review.
- **Bias awareness** — avoid anchoring, overconfidence, and recency bias.
- **Consistency** — same standards for identical risks.

Judgment without discipline produces inconsistent, unprofitable results.

## Summary

- The principles are **selection, classification, anti-selection control, pricing discipline, adequacy, portfolio balance, documentation, and fairness**.
- **Adverse selection** is controlled through screening, data, classification, and restriction.
- **Classification** must be actuarially supported and legally permissible.
- **Risk appetite** and **authority** translate principles into rules.
- **Moral hazard and fraud** are managed through verification and investigation.
- **Judgment** is valuable only within a disciplined framework.
