# Glossary of Terms

This glossary defines key insurance and underwriting terms used throughout the handbook. Definitions are plain-English first, with technical detail where relevant.

## A

- **Accepted risk** — A risk that an underwriter agrees to insure, typically within the insurer's appetite and guidelines.
- **Additional insured** — A person or organization added to a policy who shares in coverage, e.g., a landlord or contractor named on a tenant's policy.
- **Adverse selection** — The tendency for higher-risk individuals or businesses to seek or retain insurance at rates that do not reflect their risk, driving up claim costs for the pool.
- **Aggregate limit** — The maximum amount a policy will pay during a policy period, regardless of the number of claims.
- **Appetite (risk appetite)** — An insurer's stated willingness to accept certain types and levels of risk.
- **Application (proposal)** — The form (paper or electronic) an applicant completes to request insurance; it becomes the basis of the policy.
- **Assignment** — Transfer of rights or duties under a policy, generally requiring insurer consent.
- **Atlas / attachment point** — The threshold at which a policy or reinsurance contract begins to respond.

## B

- **Bind / binder** — To accept a risk and provide immediate temporary coverage, often before the formal policy is issued.
- **Broker** — An intermediary who represents the insured in placing insurance with insurers (contrast with agent).
- **Burning cost** — The historical loss cost of an exposure, often used in pricing.
- **Businessowners policy (BOP)** — A package policy combining property, liability, and other coverages for small businesses.

## C

- **Capacity** — The maximum amount of risk an insurer or market can accept, influenced by capital, appetite, and reinsurance.
- **Ceding commission** — A payment from a reinsurer to a ceding insurer covering acquisition and administration costs.
- **Certificate of insurance** — A document evidencing that a policy is in force and summarizing key coverages; it is not a policy.
- **Claim** — A demand for payment under a policy following a loss.
- **Class rating** — Pricing based on broad categories (classes) of risk rather than individual risk characteristics.
- **Combined ratio** — Loss ratio plus expense ratio; a value below 100% indicates an underwriting profit.
- **Concurrent causation** — Two or more perils acting together to cause a loss; coverage depends on the causes and policy wording.
- **Condition precedent** — A condition that must be satisfied before coverage or a claim is payable.
- **Contribution** — The principle allowing insurers sharing a loss to apportion payment.
- **Coverage part** — A distinct insuring agreement within a policy, e.g., "Property Coverage Part".
- **Covenant** — (In insurance context) a promise or obligation in an application or policy.

## D

- **Declarations page (dec page)** — The policy page stating who is insured, what is covered, limits, deductible, premium, and policy period.
- **Deductible** — The amount the insured pays out of pocket before the insurer pays.
- **Defense costs** — Legal costs the insurer pays to defend the insured against a covered claim.
- **Direct writer** — An insurer that distributes through its own employees or affiliates, not independent agents.
- **Duty of disclosure** — The insured's obligation to disclose material facts (see utmost good faith).

## E

- **Endorsement** — A written amendment to a policy adding, deleting, or changing coverages, conditions, or limits.
- **Earned premium** — The portion of premium that corresponds to the expired part of the policy period.
- **Experience** — The loss history of an insured or class, used in rating.
- **Exposure** — The unit subject to insurance, e.g., payroll, sales, number of vehicles, or property value.
- **Exposure base** — The measure used to derive premium, e.g., gross sales for general liability or payroll for workers compensation.
- **Express warranty** — A condition stated in the policy or application that must be true for coverage to apply.

## F

- **Facultative reinsurance** — Reinsurance negotiated individually for a specific risk.
- **File and use** — A rate filing regime where rates may be used upon filing, subject to later review.
- **First-party claim** — A claim by the insured against their own insurer (e.g., property damage to own premises).
- **Fronting** — Arrangement where a licensed insurer issues a policy but transfers the risk to a reinsurer (often used for surplus lines or captive programs).

## G

- **Garaging address** — The location where a vehicle is principally kept, a key auto rating factor.
- **General liability** — Coverage for third-party bodily injury and property damage claims against the insured.

## H

- **Hard market** — A market with reduced capacity, higher rates, and stricter terms.
- **Hazard** — A condition that increases the likelihood or severity of loss (physical, moral, morale, legal).

## I

- **Indemnity** — Restoring the insured to the financial position they were in before the loss, no better and no worse.
- **Inspection** — Physical or virtual survey of a risk to verify information and identify hazards.
- **Insurable interest** — A financial or legal interest in the subject matter such that loss would cause financial harm (required to insure).
- **Insured** — The party protected under a policy.
- **Insurer** — The company providing insurance.

## L

- **Limit (limit of liability)** — The maximum amount the insurer will pay under a coverage part.
- **Loss** — Damage, injury, or liability giving rise to a claim; also the amount paid or reserved.
- **Loss control** — Measures to prevent or reduce losses (engineering, safety programs, inspections).
- **Loss ratio** — Incurred losses divided by earned premium.
- **Loss run** — A report of the insured's loss history from the insurer.

## M

- **Moral hazard** — Risk arising from the insured's character or integrity, e.g., dishonesty or fraudulent claims.
- **Morale hazard** — Risk arising from carelessness or indifference, e.g., not maintaining premises.
- **Material fact** — A fact that would influence a prudent underwriter in accepting or pricing a risk; must be disclosed.
- **MPL (maximum possible loss)** — The worst-case loss that could occur to a single risk.

## N

- **Named insured** — The person or entity specified as insured in the declarations.
- **Named peril policy** — A policy covering only perils explicitly listed (contrast with all-risk/open perils).
- **Non-renewal** — The insurer's decision not to renew an expiring policy.

## O

- **Occurrence** — An accident or event causing bodily injury or property damage, including continuous or repeated exposure to conditions.
- **Open perils (all risk)** — Coverage for all risks of loss not otherwise excluded.
- **Other insurance** — Provisions that allocate payment when more than one policy covers a loss.

## P

- **Peril** — A cause of loss, e.g., fire, windstorm, theft.
- **Policy** — The contract of insurance.
- **Policy period** — The period of coverage, stated in the declarations.
- **Premium** — The amount paid for insurance coverage.
- **Proposal form** — The application form the insured completes (common in UK markets).
- **Proximate cause** — The dominant and effective cause of a loss, used to determine coverage.
- **PRP (prudent reinsurer principle)** — A principle that the reinsurer follows the fortunes of the ceding insurer.

## R

- **Rate** — The cost of insurance per unit of exposure.
- **Rate making** — The actuarial process of setting rates.
- **Reinstatement** — Restoration of limits after a claim, often for an additional premium.
- **Reinsurance** — Insurance purchased by an insurer to transfer part of its risk.
- **Renewal** — Continuation of a policy for another term.
- **Retention** — The amount of risk an insurer keeps before reinsurance applies (also a deductible under a reinsurance contract).
- **Risk** — (1) The chance of loss; (2) the subject of insurance (a building, business, person).

## S

- **Schedule rating** — Adjustments to rate for individual risk characteristics, e.g., credits or debits for management quality.
- **SIU (Special Investigation Unit)** — A claims unit investigating suspected fraud.
- **Soft market** — A market with abundant capacity, lower rates, and broader terms.
- **Subrogation** — The insurer's right to recover from a third party who caused the loss after paying the insured.
- **Surety** — A three-party contract under which a surety guarantees performance of an obligation (bond).

## T

- **Third-party claim** — A claim made against the insured by someone else, covered under liability insurance.
- **Treaty reinsurance** — A standing agreement covering a class or portfolio of risks, not individually negotiated.
- **TUG (terms, unique circumstances, guarantee)** — (In UK) terms and special conditions of a policy.

## U

- **Underwriter** — The professional who evaluates and accepts or declines risk and sets terms/prices.
- **Underwriting** — The process of evaluating, selecting, classifying, and pricing risk.
- **Underwriting year** — An accounting period based on when policies incept, used to measure performance.
- **Utmost good faith (uberrimae fidei)** — The duty of both parties to a contract of insurance to act honestly and disclose all material facts.

## V

- **Valuation** — The basis for measuring a loss (e.g., actual cash value or replacement cost).

## W

- **Warranty** — A condition of the policy that must be strictly compiled with; breach can void coverage.
- **Workers compensation** — Statutory coverage for work-related injury or illness, paid regardless of fault.

## Z

- **Zone rating** — Geographic rating based on territory loss experience.
