# 5.1 Pricing Fundamentals

## Overview

**Pricing** is the determination of the rate (and therefore premium) that an insurer must charge to cover expected losses, expenses, capital costs, and profit — while remaining competitive. Pricing is distinct from **rating**: rating applies the approved rate structure to a specific risk; pricing sets the level of the rate structure itself.

## The Insurance Pricing Equation

The basic pricing identity:

```
Premium = Pure Premium ÷ (1 − Expense Ratio − Profit & Contingency Loading)

Pure Premium = Loss Cost = (Frequency × Severity)
```

| Component | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Pure premium** | The portion of premium needed to pay losses (and loss adjustment expenses) |
| **Frequency** | Expected number of claims per exposure unit |
| **Severity** | Expected average cost per claim |
| **Expense ratio** | Acquisition, general, and premium taxes as % of premium |
| **Profit & contingency** | Target underwriting profit and provisions for deviation |

### Loss Ratio Form

```
Indicated Rate Change = (Experience Loss Ratio − Target Loss Ratio) ÷ Experience Loss Ratio × 100%
```

## Ratio Concepts

| Ratio | Formula | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Loss ratio** | Incurred Losses ÷ Earned Premium | Claims (% of premium) |
| **LAE ratio** | Loss Adjustment Expense ÷ Earned Premium | Claims handling cost |
| **Expense ratio** | Acquisition + General Expense ÷ Written Premium | Operating cost |
| **Combined ratio** | Loss + LAE + Expense Ratio | Underwriting profitability (<100% = profit) |
| **Operating ratio** | Combined Ratio − Investment Income Ratio | Net of investment earnings |
| **Retention ratio** | (Written − Ceded) ÷ Written | Net premium |

### Combined Ratio Example

```
Loss ratio (incl. LAE):        65.0%
Expense ratio:                 30.0%
Combined ratio:                95.0%  ← profitable
Investment income ratio:        3.0%
Operating ratio:               92.0%
```

A combined ratio below 100% means an **underwriting profit**; above 100% means losses and expenses exceed premium, offset only by investment income or capital.

## Components of Rate

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| **Expected (pure) loss cost** | The actuarial best estimate of losses per exposure |
| **Loss adjustment expense (LAE)** | Allocated (ALAE) and unallocated (ULAE) claims costs |
| **Acquisition expense** | Commissions, distribution costs |
| **General expense** | Overhead, other operating costs |
| **Premium taxes and assessments** | Statutory levies |
| **Risk / profit margin** | Compensation for uncertainty and capital |

## Ratemaking vs. Pricing

| Concept | Description |
|---|---|
| **Ratemaking** | Actuarial determination of the rate level for a class/book (manuals, filings) |
| **Pricing** | Business-level decision on rates: discounting, underwriting judgment, market conditions |
| **Rating** | Application of the approved rate structure to an individual risk |

The **underwriter prices individual risks** within the framework the **actuary** develops; discipline requires the underwriter to respect the indicated rate unless justified.

## Rate Levels and Exposures

- **Rate** is premium per unit of exposure:

| Line | Exposure Unit |
|---|---|
| **Commercial property** | \$100 of value (building/contents) |
| **Auto (liability)** | Car year |
| **General liability** | \$1,000 of receipts / \$100 of payroll |
| **Workers comp** | \$100 of payroll |
| **Life/health** | \$1,000 of sum assured |

## The Underwriting Cycle and Pricing

| Phase | Pricing Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Soft** | Rates fall below indicated; capacity is ample; loss ratios deteriorate |
| **Hard** | Rates rise; capacity tightens; underwriting profit returns |
| **Discipline** | Maintain adequate rates through the cycle; don't chase volume |

## Summary

- Premium = pure premium plus **expenses, taxes, profit, and contingency**.
- The **combined ratio** measures underwriting profitability.
- **Frequency and severity** drive loss cost; exposure bases convert loss cost to rates.
- **Ratemaking** sets the manual; **rating** applies it; **pricing** adjusts for judgment and market.
- **Pricing discipline** through the cycle protects solvency.
