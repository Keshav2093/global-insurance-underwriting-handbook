# 1.3 Insurance as a Business

## The Insurer's Business Model

An insurer receives premiums and pays claims. Its long-term viability depends on:

1. **Underwriting results** — premiums less claims and expenses.
2. **Investment income** — returns on the funds held (reserves, unearned premium, capital) between the receipt of premium and the payment of claims.
3. **Expense control** — acquisition, administration, and claims-handling efficiency.

## The Two Sources of Profit

| Source | Description |
|---|---|
| **Underwriting profit** | Premiums earned minus losses incurred minus expenses incurred |
| **Investment income** | Returns from investing the "float" — the funds held between receiving premiums and paying claims |

In property-casualty insurance, the industry as a whole has historically earned most of its profit from **investment income**, with underwriting results fluctuating between profit and loss around the **underwriting cycle**.

## Key Financial Measures

### Loss Ratio

> Loss Ratio = Incurred Losses ÷ Earned Premium

Incurred losses include claims paid (or payable) **plus** changes in loss reserves. A loss ratio above its target indicates the price is insufficient for the risk.

### Expense Ratio

> Expense Ratio = Underwriting Expenses ÷ Written Premium

Expenses include acquisition costs (commissions, brokerage), general and administrative costs, and sometimes premium taxes and other acquisition-related items.

### Combined Ratio

> Combined Ratio = Loss Ratio + Expense Ratio

- **Below 100%**: underwriting profit.
- **Above 100%**: underwriting loss.
- The **combined ratio excluding investment income** is the "trade" or "operating" ratio most commonly used to judge underwriting performance.

### Other Ratios

| Measure | Formula | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Operating ratio** | Combined ratio − investment income ratio | Measures overall underwriting-plus-investment performance |
| **Reserve ratio** | Change in reserves ÷ earned premium | Flags reserve adequacy trends |
| **Capacity** | Premium volume relative to surplus/capital | Regulatory and security measure |

## Underwriting Profit vs. Investment Income

| | Underwriting Profit | Investment Income |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Premium pricing and loss control | Investing the float |
| Volatility | Cycles with market conditions | Relatively stable |
| Control | Underwriter influence is direct | Investment team influence |
| Examples of failure | Persistent soft-market underpricing | Duration mismatch, credit losses |

## The Underwriting Cycle

Insurance markets alternate between **hard** and **soft** phases:

| Phase | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| **Soft market** | Abundant capacity; falling rates; broadened terms; relaxed underwriting; more competition |
| **Hard market** | Reduced capacity; rising rates; narrowed terms; tighter underwriting; fewer markets |

### Causes of the Cycle

- **Capacity and capital** — capital flows in when results are good, expanding capacity; capacity contracts after catastrophe losses or poor results.
- **Prior-year reserve development** — poor prior underwriting years emerge later, prompting tightening.
- **Market behavior** — competition and the lag between rate changes and loss experience.
- **Catastrophes** — sudden shocks reduce capital, raising rates.

### Underwriting Implications

- In a **soft market**, underwriters face pressure to relax standards; those who resist preserve quality.
- In a **hard market**, discipline is easier but chasing price can still attract adverse selection.
- The best underwriters maintain consistent standards across the cycle and manage **rate adequacy** rather than chasing volume.

## Financial Statements of an Insurer

### Balance Sheet Essentials

| Item | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Assets** | Invested assets, cash, reinsurance recoverables, premiums receivable |
| **Liabilities** | Loss reserves, unearned premium reserve (UPR), outstanding claims, reinsurance payable |
| **Surplus (equity)** | Assets minus liabilities; the cushion that supports writing risk |

### The Loss Reserve

The **loss reserve** is the estimated amount an insurer must pay for claims that have occurred but are not yet settled:

- **Case reserves** — set per reported claim.
- **IBNR (Incurred But Not Reported)** — provision for claims not yet reported.
- **IBNER (Incurred But Not Enough Reported)** — provision for changes in the value of already-reported claims.

Reserving is inherently uncertain. Under-reserving inflates reported profits today and creates losses tomorrow; over-reserving depresses today's results but builds strength.

### Unearned Premium Reserve (UPR)

Premium is earned over the policy period. At any date, the portion of written premium not yet earned is held in the UPR. If a policy is cancelled, the UPR funds the return premium.

## How Premium Is Built (Preview)

The premium must cover:

1. **Expected losses** — the predicted cost of claims.
2. **Expenses** — acquisition, administration, claims handling.
3. **Risk margin / profit** — compensation for bearing risk and the cost of capital.

## Business Structures

| Structure | Description |
|---|---|
| **Stock insurer** | Owned by shareholders; profit may be distributed as dividends |
| **Mutual insurer** | Owned by policyholders; profits may be returned as dividends or retained |
| **Lloyd's / syndicate** | Capital provided by members (Names) pooling risk in syndicates |
| **Captive insurer** | An insurer owned by its parent to insure the parent's risks |
| **Reciprocal insurer** | An unincorporated mutual: policyholders insure each other through an attorney-in-fact |

## Regulation and Solvency

Because insurers hold policyholder money and bear long-tail risk, they are heavily regulated for **solvency**:

- **Capital adequacy** — minimum capital and surplus (US risk-based capital; UK Solvency II).
- **Rate and form regulation** — many states require filing/approval of rates and forms.
- **Market conduct** — fair treatment of policyholders, claims handling, and advertising.
- **Guaranty/solvency funds** — US state guaranty associations pay claims of insolvent insurers; UK has the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS).

## Summary

- Insurance is a business of **premium in, claims out**, judged on combined ratio and investment return.
- The **combined ratio** is the headline underwriting measure.
- The **underwriting cycle** drives market conditions and underwriting discipline.
- **Reserves** are the largest liability and the key source of uncertainty.
- Insurers are **regulated for solvency** to protect policyholders.
