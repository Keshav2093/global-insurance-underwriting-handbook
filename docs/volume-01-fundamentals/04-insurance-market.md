# 1.4 The Insurance Market

## Market Structure

The insurance market is the system through which risk is transferred from those who face it (buyers) to those who can bear it (insurers, and ultimately reinsurers and capital markets). It comprises:

- **Buyers** — individuals, families, businesses, and organizations.
- **Intermediaries** — agents and brokers who connect buyers with insurers.
- **Insurers** — primary carriers that underwrite and issue policies.
- **Reinsurers** — insurers of insurers.
- **Specialist markets** — e.g., Lloyd's of London, regional specialty carriers, surplus lines markets.
- **Regulators and market infrastructure** — insurance departments, rating agencies, advisory/statistical organizations, exchanges.

## Buyers

### Personal Lines Buyers

- Individuals and families.
- Purchase standardized products (auto, home, umbrella) through agents, brokers, direct writers, or online.
- Are relatively price-sensitive; behavior is regulated strongly (disclosure, fairness, market conduct).

### Commercial Lines Buyers

- Small businesses: often buy packaged products (BOP) through agents.
- Middle market: more customized; use of brokers.
- Large/global risks: heavily engineered programs, captives, and layered reinsurance; often risk managers negotiate.

### The Risk Manager

Large organizations employ **risk managers** who:

- Identify and evaluate risks.
- Decide on retention vs. transfer.
- Purchase insurance and administer captives.
- Manage claims and loss control programs.

## Intermediaries: Agents and Brokers

| Feature | Agent | Broker |
|---|---|---|
| Represents | The insurer (in most agency agreements) | The insured (in most broker arrangements) |
| Authority | May bind coverage on the insurer's behalf (with authority) | Usually no authority to bind; must place with insurer |
| Market | Single insurer or a few | Multiple markets; can shop |
| Remuneration | Commission from the insurer | Commission and/or fee from the insured |

### Types of Agents

- **Captive/exclusive agents** — represent one insurer.
- **Independent agents** — represent multiple insurers; own the renewal (the "book").
- **Managing general agents (MGAs)** — have delegated authority to underwrite, bind, and settle claims for an insurer, often in specialty lines.

### The Broker's Role

Brokers:

- Analyze the client's exposures.
- Prepare the submission and market it to insurers.
- Negotiate terms and price.
- Advise on coverage gaps.
- Assist with claims and policy administration.

In the Lloyd's and London market, **brokers** are central; access to the market is generally through a Lloyd's broker.

## Insurers

### Categories of Primary Insurers

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| **Direct writers** | Sell through employees, online, or call centres without independent agents |
| **Agency insurers** | Distribute through independent or captive agents |
| **Mutuals** | Policyholder-owned |
| **Stock insurers** | Shareholder-owned |
| **Specialty insurers** | Focus on niche lines (e.g., marine, aviation, professional liability) |
| **Surplus lines insurers** | Non-admitted carriers writing risks that admitted carriers decline |
| **Captives** | Owned by the insured to self-insure |

### Admitted vs. Non-Admitted (Surplus Lines) — US

| | Admitted | Non-Admitted (Surplus Lines) |
|---|---|---|
| Licensed in state | Yes | No (eligible but not licensed) |
| Rate/form regulation | Subject to state filing/approval | Exempt from rate/form filing |
| Guaranty fund protection | Covered by state guaranty fund | Generally not covered |
| Use | Standard risks | Unusual, high-risk, or high-capacity needs through a licensed surplus lines broker with due diligence |

### UK Market

- **FCA/PRA-authorized** insurers.
- **Lloyd's** is a specialist market of syndicates underwriting through a single license.
- **Company market** — authorized insurers under Solvency II.
- **London Market** — the insurance and reinsurance market centered on Lloyd's and company market underwriters (marine, aviation, energy, specialty).

## Reinsurers

**Reinsurance** is insurance purchased by an insurer to protect against losses that exceed its capacity or appetite. Reinsurers supply:

- **Capacity** — allowing primary insurers to write larger lines.
- **Stability** — smoothing the impact of catastrophes and large losses.
- **Expertise** — modeling, claims, and technical insight.

### Types of Reinsurance

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Facultative** | Individually negotiated per risk; used for large or unusual exposures |
| **Treaty** | Standing agreement covering a class of business; automatic obligation |
| **Proportional (quota share, surplus share)** | The reinsurer shares premiums and losses pro rata |
| **Excess of loss (per risk, per event, aggregate)** | The reinsurer pays losses above a retention up to a limit |

### Reinsurance Structures

- **Quota share (QS):** the ceding company cedes a fixed percentage of all risks in a class.
- **Surplus share:** ceding is based on the amount of risk above the ceding company's retention.
- **Excess of loss (XOL):** reinsurer applies above a loss retention; typical for catastrophe and casualty programs.
- **Stop loss (aggregate):** caps the ceding company's total loss ratio for a period.

Underwriting capacity is typically expressed as the insurer's **net retention** (the amount it can keep for its own account) plus **reinsurance capacity**.

## Lloyd's of London

Lloyd's is a specialist insurance market, not an insurance company:

- **Syndicates** — groups of capital providers (members) underwrite business.
- **Managing agents** — run the syndicates.
- **Brokers** — access to Lloyd's is through approved brokers.
- **The Society of Lloyd's** — provides governance, licensing, and the central fund that protects policyholders if members default.

Lloyd's is a leading market for marine, aviation, energy, political risk, and other specialty lines, and is closely associated with the London Market.

## Advisory and Infrastructure Organizations

| Organization | Role |
|---|---|
| **ISO (Insurance Services Office)** | Develops policy forms and statistical data in the US |
| **NCCI (National Council on Compensation Insurance)** | Workers compensation ratemaking in most US states |
| **AM Best, S&P, Moody's, Fitch** | Financial strength ratings of insurers |
| **NAIC (US)** | Coordinates state regulators; model laws |
| **A.M. Best / Lloyd's market associations** | Market data and standards |
| **Actuarial bodies** | Standard-setting for reserving and pricing (CAS, SOA, IFoA) |

## Market Segments

### By Distribution Channel

- **Agency** (independent/captive)
- **Broker**
- **Direct** (online, call centre, worksite)
- **Bancassurance** (banks selling insurance)
- **MGAs** (specialist underwriting with delegated authority)

### By Customer

- **Personal lines**
- **Small commercial**
- **Middle market**
- **Large accounts / national accounts**
- **Specialty** (marine, aviation, energy, professional, cyber, etc.)

### By Geography

- **Domestic** — within one country.
- **Cross-border** — insurers operating in multiple jurisdictions.
- **Global** — large commercial and reinsurance programs spanning the world.

## Market Conduct and Fairness

Regulators impose **market conduct** rules to ensure buyers are treated fairly:

- Clear and fair advertising and sales practices.
- Disclosure of material terms and coverage.
- Timely and fair claims handling.
- Respect for privacy of personal data.
- No unfair discrimination or redlining.

Market conduct is examined in detail in the [US Market](../market-us/index.md) and [UK Market](../market-uk/index.md) volumes.

## Summary

- The market has distinct roles: buyers, intermediaries, insurers, reinsurers, and infrastructure.
- **Agents** represent insurers; **brokers** represent insureds.
- **Reinsurance** provides capacity and stability and shapes insurers' net retentions.
- **Lloyd's** is a specialist market for complex risks.
- **Regulators** ensure solvency and fair conduct.
