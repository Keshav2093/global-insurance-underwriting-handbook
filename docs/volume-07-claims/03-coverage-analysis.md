# 7.3 Coverage Analysis

## Overview

**Coverage analysis** determines whether a claim is covered by the policy. It requires reading the policy as a whole, applying legal principles of interpretation, and documenting the reasoning. Errors in coverage analysis are among the most expensive claims failures.

## The Coverage Question

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| **Is the policy in force?** | Effective dates, status, retroactive date |
| **Is the event covered?** | Insuring agreement trigger |
| **Is an exclusion applicable?** | Pollution, intentional act, wear and tear |
| **Are conditions met?** | Notice, cooperation, suit requirement |
| **What is the limit?** | Per occurrence, aggregate, sub-limits |
| **Does a coverage trigger apply?** | Occurrence, claims-made, accident |

## Policy Interpretation Principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Plain meaning** | Ordinary meaning of policy language |
| **Ambiguity resolves against drafter** | Against the insurer (typically) |
| **Reasonable expectations** | Insured's reasonable understanding |
| **Exclusions strictly construed** | Narrow reading of exclusions |
| **Whole policy reading** | No clause read in isolation |
| **Trade usage** | Terms have industry meanings |

## Structure of Analysis

```
1. Identify the applicable coverage parts
2. Apply the insuring agreement (is the loss within it?)
3. Check definitions (occurrence, insured, property damage)
4. Check exclusions (does any apply?)
5. Check conditions (notice, cooperation, suit)
6. Determine limit, deductible, and trigger
7. State the conclusion with reasoning
```

## Common Coverage Issues

| Issue | Handling |
|---|---|
| **Occurrence vs. claims-made** | Trigger date and retroactive date |
| **Intentional act** | Excluded; coverage for accidents only |
| **Pollution exclusion** | Absolute vs. sudden-and-accidental |
| **Contractual liability** | Assumed liability; insured contract carve-out |
| **Employee injuries** | WC/employers liability vs. GL |
| **Mold/fungus** | Sub-limits and exclusions |
| **Business auto use** | Hired/non-owned vs. owned auto |

## Reservation of Rights

When coverage is **uncertain**, the insurer issues a **reservation of rights (ROR)**:

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| **Notice** | Advises of potential coverage issues |
| **Investigation** | Insurer continues investigating |
| **Defense** | May still defend the insured |
| **Rights preserved** | Insurer may later deny coverage |

A ROR **does not deny coverage** — it preserves the insurer's rights while investigating.

## Declaratory Judgment

- The insurer may seek a **court determination** of coverage.
- Used where coverage depends on disputed facts or law.
- May run alongside the underlying claim.

## Coverage Determination Outcomes

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Accept** | Claim covered; proceed to valuation and payment |
| **Deny** | Not covered; provide clear explanation to insured |
| **Reserve rights** | Coverage unresolved; continue investigation |
| **Partially cover** | Some elements covered; others not |
| **Defense obligation only** | Indemnity uncertain; defend under ROR |

## Coverage Documentation

The coverage memo should record:

- Policy references (forms, endorsements, conditions).
- The facts as known.
- Application of insuring agreement, exclusions, conditions.
- Legal authority where relevant.
- The decision and basis.
- Communications with the insured.

## Bad Faith Exposure

| Risk | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Wrong denial** | Breach of contract; bad faith damages |
| **Unreasonable delay** | Statutory penalties; interest |
| **Inadequate investigation before denial** | Bad faith finding |
| **Failure to explain denial** | Regulatory complaint |
| **Failed ROR** | Waiver or estoppel arguments |

## Summary

- Coverage analysis answers **is this claim within the policy?**
- Use **insuring agreement, definitions, exclusions, conditions, limits, triggers**.
- Follow **interpretation principles** — plain meaning, contra proferentem, reasonable expectations.
- **Reservation of rights** protects the insurer during investigation.
- **Documented, prompt, and fair decisions** avoid bad faith.
