# 3.8 Life, Accident & Health (Commercial)

## Overview

Businesses purchase a range of **life, accident, and health (LA&H)** products to protect their employees, their owners, and the business itself. These products fall into two broad categories:

- **Group benefits** — medical, life, disability, and accident coverages for employees.
- **Business protection** — key person, buy-sell, and business expense coverages.

This chapter covers the commercial LA&H products an underwriter meets, with emphasis on underwriting considerations.

## Group Benefits

| Product | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Group life** | Term life for employees, often a multiple of salary (e.g., 1–2×) |
| **Group medical/health** | Medical expense coverage for employees and their families |
| **Group short-term disability (STD)** | Wage replacement during short absences |
| **Group long-term disability (LTD)** | Wage replacement for extended disability |
| **Group accident (AD&D)** | Accidental death/dismemberment benefits |
| **Vision and dental** | Supplemental benefits |
| **Voluntary (worksite) benefits** | Employee-paid supplements (life, critical illness, accident) |

### Underwriting Group Benefits

Group underwriting is fundamentally different from individual underwriting:

- **No individual medical selection** for most groups (guaranteed issue).
- The **group as a whole** is rated on:
  - Participation rate (percentage of eligible employees).
  - Employer size and stability.
  - Industry and occupation class.
  - Claims history (experience-rated groups).
  - Employee demographics (age, gender, salary distribution).

### Participation and Anti-Selection

- **Guaranteed issue** requires high participation (e.g., 75%+ of eligible).
- **Low participation** invites **anti-selection** — only unhealthy employees enroll.
- **Voluntary products** are especially exposed; insurers set participation minimums.

### Group Underwriting Tiers

| Tier | Description |
|---|---|
| **Fully insured** | Insurer assumes the risk; premium fixed for the period |
| **Experience-rated** | Large groups' premiums reflect their own claims (loss ratio) |
| **Self-funded** | Employer funds claims; insurer provides ASO (administrative services only) and stop-loss |
| **Stop-loss** | Insurer reimburses claims above an attachment point (specific and aggregate) |

## Stop-Loss Insurance

**Stop-loss (excess medical)** covers self-funded employers:

- **Specific stop-loss** — reimburses claims above a per-person attachment (e.g., \$100,000).
- **Aggregate stop-loss** — reimburses claims above the expected total for the group.
- **Underwriting factors:** group size, demographics, plan design, prior claims (rx and medical), financial condition of the employer.

Stop-loss underwriting is data-heavy:

- **Prior claims** (medical and pharmacy) for 2–3 years.
- **Census data** — age/gender distribution.
- **Plan design** — deductibles, out-of-pocket maximums.
- **Laser underwriting** — specific high-cost individuals are "lasered" (excluded or lowered attachment).

## Key Person Insurance

**Key person (keyman) insurance** covers the business against financial loss from the death or disability of a **key person** — an owner, executive, or essential employee whose loss would damage the business.

- The **business** is the owner and beneficiary.
- **Coverage:** term or whole life on the key person; disability income or buy-out cover.
- **Underwriting:** the key person's health and income; the business's financial dependence on them; the amount needed (revenue loss, replacement, debt).

### Underwriting Data

- Financial statements of the business.
- Description of the key person's role and value.
- Insurance needs calculation (replacement, debt, loss of profits).
- Key person's medical/lifestyle underwriting (as individual life).

## Buy-Sell (Shareholder Protection)

**Buy-sell agreements** fund the purchase of a deceased or disabled owner's shares:

- **Cross-purchase** — remaining owners each insure the others; proceeds fund share purchase.
- **Entity purchase** — the company insures each owner; proceeds fund company purchase of the deceased's shares.
- **Funding:** life insurance and disability buy-out policies on each owner.

### Underwriting Considerations

- **Valuation** — the agreement must specify how shares are valued.
- **Funding amount** — insurance must match the buy-sell obligation.
- **Proceeds use** — must be documented to avoid taxable or unintended outcomes.
- **Individual underwriting** — each owner's health and lifestyle.

## Business Travel Accident and Accident Products

- **Business travel accident (BTA)** — covers employees on business trips (death, dismemberment, medical).
- **Group AD&D** — accidental death/dismemberment for employees.
- **Occupational accident** — for non-subscriber employers or high-hazard trades, provides benefits similar to WC in some jurisdictions.
- **Underwriting:** travel patterns, destination risk, occupations, and participation.

## Voluntary / Worksite Products

Employees pay premiums (often via payroll deduction) for:

- Term life.
- Critical illness.
- Accident.
- Hospital indemnity.
- Cancer or specified disease.

Small premium amounts can support small groups. Insurers rely on **participation thresholds** and simplified underwriting (health questions) to control anti-selection.

## Underwriting Data and Governance

| Topic | Data / Control |
|---|---|
| **Census data** | Age, gender, salary, location |
| **Claims data** | Prior claims/usage (medical, rx) |
| **Plan design** | Benefits, cost-sharing, networks |
| **Participation** | Enrollment percentages |
| **Financial** | Employer financials for self-funded |
| **Compliance** | HIPAA/ERISA (US); FCA conduct (UK) |
| **Reinsurance** | Catastrophic and morbidity cover |

## Compliance and Conduct

- **US:** ERISA governs employer plans; HIPAA privacy; ACA market reforms; state stop-loss and product regulation.
- **UK:** FCA regulation; consumer duty and fair value for group risk products; advised distribution.

## Summary

- Commercial LA&H includes **group benefits** (life, medical, disability, accident) and **business protection** (key person, buy-sell).
- **Group underwriting** rates the group as a whole; participation control prevents anti-selection.
- **Stop-loss** protects self-funded employers; underwriting is data-heavy.
- **Key person and buy-sell** are individually underwritten on the insured lives, with the business as owner.
- **Compliance** (ERISA, HIPAA, FCA) shapes products and governance.
