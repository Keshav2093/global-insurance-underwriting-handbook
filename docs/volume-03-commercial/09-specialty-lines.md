# 3.9 Specialty Lines

## Overview

**Specialty lines** is the umbrella term for insurance coverages that do not fit neatly into standard property, liability, auto, or WC lines. They often require specialized forms, specialized underwriting expertise, and a distinct market (surplus lines, London market, MGAs).

This chapter surveys the principal specialty lines and their underwriting considerations.

## Categories of Specialty Lines

| Category | Coverages |
|---|---|
| **Marine** | Ocean marine (hull, cargo, freight) and inland marine |
| **Aviation** | Aircraft hull, aviation liability, airport liability, space |
| **Surety** | Performance bonds, payment bonds, fidelity bonds |
| **Professional liability** | Medical malpractice, lawyers' E&O, architects, accountants, brokers |
| **Directors & Officers (D&O)** | Management liability |
| **Cyber** | Data breach, cyber liability, network security |
| **Environmental** | Pollution liability, remediation |
| **Energy** | Onshore/offshore energy, power plants |
| **Financial lines** | D&O, E&O, crime, employment practices, fiduciary |
| **Construction** | Builders risk, project-specific liability, wrap-ups |
| **Political risk / trade credit** | Expropriation, contract frustration, credit default |

## Ocean Marine

| Coverage | Underwriting Focus |
|---|---|
| **Hull** | Vessel value, age, type, flag, condition, P&I club, navigation limits |
| **Cargo** | Commodity, packing, route, carrier, port conditions, declaration basis |
| **Freight** | Length of voyage, vessel suitability |
| **Protection & Indemnity (P&I)** | Crew, liability to third parties, pollution; often through P&I clubs |
| **Marine liability** | Wreck removal, pollution, collision (running-down clause) |

Key considerations: **vessel condition surveys, crew competence, route hazards (piracy, war zones), cargo packing and fragility, and cargo values declared.**

## Inland Marine

- **Domestic transit** — goods in transit within the country.
- **Bailees' customers** — property in the insured's care (dry cleaners, repair shops).
- **Contractors' equipment** — machinery and tools at job sites.
- **Builders risk** — construction in progress.
- **Installation floaters** — equipment being installed.
- **Electronic data processing (EDP)** — computers and media.
- **Accounts receivable / valuable papers** — records and receivables.
- **Fine arts / collections** — for businesses.
- **Motor truck cargo** — goods carried by truckers.

Inland marine is one of the **fastest-growing and most flexible** lines because it covers property that is **movable** or **in transit**, which the standard property policy does not.

## Aviation

| Coverage | Exposure |
|---|---|
| **Aircraft hull** | Physical damage to the aircraft |
| **Liability** | Bodily injury/property damage to third parties and passengers |
| **Airport premises** | Ground operations, hangars, fuel |
| **Products liability** | Manufacturer liability |
| **Space** | Satellite launch and in-orbit |

Underwriting factors: pilot qualifications and hours, aircraft type and maintenance, use (private/business/charter), flight routes, ground risk, and passengers.

## Surety

Surety is not insurance in the traditional sense — it is a **three-party guarantee**:

| Party | Role |
|---|---|
| **Principal** | The party required to perform (contractor, principal) |
| **Obligee** | The party receiving the guarantee (owner, government) |
| **Surety** | The guarantor (insurer) |

### Types of Bonds

| Bond | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Bid bond** | Guarantees the bidder will enter the contract if awarded |
| **Performance bond** | Guarantees completion of the contract |
| **Payment bond** | Guarantees payment to subcontractors and suppliers |
| **Maintenance bond** | Guarantees workmanship for a period |
| **License & permit bonds** | Compliance with regulations |
| **Court bonds** | Appeal, fiduciary, injunction bonds |
| **Fidelity bonds** | Employee dishonesty (commercial crime) |

### Underwriting Surety

- The surety underwrites **the principal's ability to perform**, not the hazard of loss.
- **Financial strength** — balance sheet, working capital, liquidity, credit.
- **Capacity** — the contractor's aggregate bond program vs. net worth.
- **Experience** — prior success on similar contracts.
- **Claims history** — completed contracts, litigation.
- **Aggregate exposure** — concentration risk on a single contractor.

Surety underwriting is a **credit underwriting** discipline; unlike insurance, the surety expects **zero loss**. A performance bond loss is a default, not an expected claim frequency event.

## Professional Liability (E&O / Malpractice)

| Market | Underwriting Focus |
|---|---|
| **Medical malpractice** | Specialty, claims history, peer review, hospital privileges, limits |
| **Lawyers' professional liability** | Practice areas (litigation, securities, real estate), claims history, firm size |
| **Accountants' E&O** | Client size, services (audit, tax, advisory), SEC exposure |
| **Architects & engineers** | Project size, discipline, contractual terms, claims history |
| **Insurance agents/brokers E&O** | Volume, lines, claims, procedures |
| **Technology E&O** | Software/services, data exposure, contractual liability |
| **Miscellaneous E&O** | Consultants of all types |

These are **claims-made** policies; underwriting focuses on **professional qualifications, risk management procedures, claims history, and exposure metrics** (revenue, headcount, project size).

## Directors & Officers (D&O) Liability

Covers **management** for claims alleging wrongful acts in the management of the company:

- **Side A** — personal coverage for directors/officers when the company cannot indemnify.
- **Side B** — reimbursement to the company for indemnifying directors/officers.
- **Side C** — securities claims against the entity (entity coverage).

Underwriting focus: **company financials, industry, governance, prior litigation, securities exposure, IPO status, and D&O claims history.**

## Cyber Insurance

Covers **data breaches, network security failures, and liability from compromised data**:

| Coverage | Description |
|---|---|
| **First-party** | Breach response (forensics, notification, credit monitoring), business interruption, data restoration, ransomware |
| **Third-party** | Network security and privacy liability, regulatory defense, media liability |
| **Extras** | Social engineering (fraud), cyber extortion, contingent business interruption |

### Cyber Underwriting

- Sector and data sensitivity (PII, PHI, payment data).
- Security controls (MFA, encryption, patching, backups).
- Prior breaches / claims.
- Revenue dependence on IT systems.
- Ransomware defense posture.
- Compliance (GDPR, HIPAA, state breach laws).
- Use of technical questionnaires or assessments.

Cyber is a **fast-evolving risk**; underwriting standards tighten as threat landscapes change (ransomware, supply-chain attacks, cloud dependence).

## Environmental and Pollution

| Coverage | Underwriting |
|---|---|
| **Property/GL pollution** | Limited cleanup, remedial action |
| **CGL pollution exclusion** | Broad exclusion; separate environmental policy |
| **Site pollution** | Known sites, remediation costs |
| **Contractors pollution** | Construction operations |
| **Environmental impairment liability (EIL)** | Gradual pollution, third-party claims |

Underwriting involves **Phase I/Phase II environmental assessments, site history, operations, waste handling, regulatory status, and financial strength for clean-up obligations.**

## Financial Lines (Other)

- **Employment Practices Liability (EPLI)** — discrimination, harassment, wrongful termination; underwriting focuses on HR practices, claims history, headcount.
- **Fidelity (Crime)** — employee dishonesty, forgery, theft; underwriting focuses on internal controls, cash handling, segregation of duties.
- **Fiduciary liability** — ERISA plans; underwriting focuses on plan administration, investment policies, and claims history.

## Construction

- **Builders risk** — property under construction; underwriting focuses on project value, schedule, contractors, fire protection during construction.
- **Contractor's liability** — GL, wrap-ups (OCIP/CCIP) for large projects.
- **Construction professional liability** — design and CM risks.
- **Surety** — performance and payment bonds for contractors.

## The Specialty Market

Specialty lines are often written in the **surplus lines / London market**:

- **Non-admitted carriers** — not regulated by state rates/forms; flexibility for unusual risks.
- **MGAs/covers** — delegated authority under clear guidelines for niche programs.
- **Reinsurance** — essential capacity for catastrophe-heavy specialty lines.

## Summary

- Specialty lines cover **marine, aviation, surety, professional, financial, cyber, environmental, energy, and construction** risks.
- Underwriting is **line-specific** and often requires specialist expertise.
- **Surety is a credit discipline** — zero-loss expectation.
- **Cyber, D&O, and professional lines** are claims-made and evolving quickly.
- **The surplus lines/London market** provides capacity for non-standard risks.
