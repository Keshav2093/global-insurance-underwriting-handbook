# 3.10 Commercial Risk Management

## Overview

**Risk management** is the discipline of identifying, analyzing, and controlling the risks that could prevent a business from achieving its objectives. For the commercial insurer, risk management is both:

- **The subject of what is insured** — managing the risks the policy covers.
- **The professional practice of the insured** — how businesses assess, retain, transfer, and mitigate risk.

This chapter frames **commercial risk management** from the perspective of both the **risk manager** (the buyer) and the **underwriter** (the insurer).

## The Risk Management Process

The classic risk management process (RIMS / ISO 31000) has five steps:

| Step | Description |
|---|---|
| 1. **Identify** | Identify all exposures to loss (property, liability, personnel, financial, strategic) |
| 2. **Analyze** | Measure frequency and severity; quantify potential impact |
| 3. **Control** | Reduce frequency (prevention) and severity (protection) |
| 4. **Finance** | Decide how to pay for losses (retention, transfer, insurance, captives) |
| 5. **Monitor** | Review and update the program continuously |

### Risk Identification

Sources of exposure:

- **Physical** — property, equipment, inventory, vehicles.
- **Liability** — third-party injury/damage, products, contracts.
- **Personnel** — employees, key people, benefits.
- **Financial** — credit, currency, interest rates, liquidity.
- **Operational** — supply chain, IT, fraud.
- **Strategic** — competition, regulation, reputation.
- **Catastrophic** — natural perils, terrorism, pandemic.

### Risk Control Techniques

| Technique | Example |
|---|---|
| **Avoidance** | Discontinuing a hazardous product line |
| **Reduction (prevention)** | Safety training, machine guards, cybersecurity controls |
| **Reduction (protection)** | Sprinklers, firewalls, backups, business continuity planning |
| **Contractual transfer** | Hold-harmless agreements, indemnities |
| **Risk retention** | Deductibles, self-insurance, captives |
| **Risk transfer (insurance)** | Purchased insurance |

## The Risk Manager's Role

The risk manager:

1. **Identifies and analyzes** risks across the enterprise.
2. **Implements loss prevention and safety programs**.
3. **Designs the insurance program** — coverages, limits, deductibles, carriers.
4. **Manages claims** — coordination with carriers, litigation control.
5. **Budgets for risk** — retentions, premiums, loss control costs.
6. **Coordinates with brokers** — submission, marketing, negotiation.
7. **Monitors coverage adequacy** — valuations, changes in exposure, endorsements.
8. **Communicates to leadership** — risk reporting, board presentations.

## Program Design

### Risk Retention vs. Transfer

| Approach | Description |
|---|---|
| **Full insurance** | Transfer all risk to insurers |
| **Deductibles** | Insured assumes losses below the deductible |
| **SIR (self-insured retention)** | Insured handles claims up to the SIR; insurer above |
| **Captive** | Owned insurer absorbs retained losses |
| **Self-insurance** | Formal self-funded program (common for WC in large employers) |

### Layered Programs

Large programs use **layers**:

- **Primary layer** — first limit (e.g., \$1M per occurrence).
- **Excess layers** — subsequent layers (e.g., \$25M excess of \$1M).
- **Umbrella** — broad excess over underlying lines.
- **Total program limit** — combined across layers.

### Program Considerations

- **Consistency** — coverage gaps between layers; drop-down provisions.
- **Carrier quality** — AM Best ratings; claims service; financial strength.
- **Territory** — global vs. domestic operations; admitted vs. non-admitted.
- **Statutory requirements** — WC, auto compulsory, EL in the UK.
- **Contractual requirements** — certificates, additional insureds, waivers of subrogation.
- **Claims control** — who handles claims at each layer.

## Captives

A **captive** is a wholly owned insurance company formed to insure the risks of its owner(s):

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Pure captive** | One owner; insures the parent's risks |
| **Group captive** | Multiple owners in similar businesses share risk |
| **Rent-a-captive** | Unrelated businesses rent capital and administration |
| **Protected cell company (PCC)** | Separate cells with segregated assets |

### Why Form a Captive?

- **Cost** — access to reinsurance at wholesale rates.
- **Tax** — premium deductions and investment returns (subject to jurisdiction rules).
- **Coverage flexibility** — cover hard-to-place risks.
- **Claims control** — direct handling and settlement management.
- **Profit retention** — underwriting profit and investment return stay in the group.
- **Risk management incentive** — measured loss costs drive safety behavior.

### Captive Administration

- **Domicile** — offshore (Bermuda, Cayman, Guernsey) or onshore (Vermont, Delaware, etc.).
- **Fronting** — a licensed insurer issues policies; the captive reinsures (fronting fee).
- **Capital and solvency** — regulated minimum capital; letters of credit.
- **Reinsurance** — captives typically buy stop-loss or quota-share reinsurance.

## Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)

**ERM** is a holistic approach to managing all risks across the organization:

| Risk Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Strategic** | Competition, M&A, regulatory change |
| **Operational** | Process failures, IT outages, fraud |
| **Financial** | Credit, market, liquidity |
| **Hazard** | Property, liability, personnel |
| **Compliance** | Law, regulation, contracts |

ERM frameworks (COSO, ISO 31000) help organizations:

- Set **risk appetite** — how much risk to take.
- **Identify and assess** risks consistently.
- **Prioritize** by likelihood and impact.
- **Implement responses** — avoid, reduce, transfer, accept.
- **Monitor and report** — board-level visibility.

## Claims Management

Effective claims management reduces cost and protects the program:

- **Prompt reporting** — early notice improves investigation and defense.
- **Reserve adequacy** — proper case reserves and IBNR.
- **Litigation management** — panel counsel, settlement authority, appeal strategy.
- **Subrogation** — recover from third parties.
- **Data analytics** — claims patterns drive safety and pricing.
- **Fraud detection** — SIU protocols.

## Loss Control and Risk Engineering

The insurer's **risk engineering** function supports underwriting and the insured:

| Service | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Property surveys** | COPE assessment, protection evaluation |
| **Safety inspections** | Workplace safety, ergonomics |
| **Business continuity reviews** | Resilience to interruption |
| **Cybersecurity assessments** | Controls, response readiness |
| **Contract reviews** | Liability transfer, additional insureds |
| **Training** | Driver safety, hot work, hazard communication |

Good loss control is the shared interest of both parties: fewer losses mean lower premiums and better outcomes.

## The Underwriter's View of Risk Management

The underwriter assesses the quality of the insured's risk management:

| Indicator | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| **Safety program** | Documented, resourced, trained | None or informal |
| **Claims management** | Prompt reporting, return-to-work | Late reporting, litigation-prone |
| **Maintenance** | Scheduled, documented | Reactive, deferred |
| **Business continuity** | Tested plans | No plan |
| **Risk ownership** | Risk manager / C-suite focus | No single owner |
| **Data** | Uses data to reduce losses | No metrics |

A strong risk management culture is a **positive underwriting factor** — it justifies broader terms and better pricing.

## Summary

- Risk management is a **five-step process**: identify, analyze, control, finance, monitor.
- The risk manager **designs the program**, **manages claims**, and **drives loss control**.
- **Retention tools** (deductibles, SIRs, captives) balance risk transfer with cost.
- **ERM** connects hazard risk to strategic, operational, and financial risks.
- The underwriter rewards **strong risk management culture** with better terms.
