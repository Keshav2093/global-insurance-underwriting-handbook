# 3.5 Workers Compensation

## Overview

**Workers compensation (WC)** is a compulsory, no-fault insurance system that provides statutory benefits to employees for work-related injuries and occupational diseases, in exchange for which employees generally surrender the right to sue the employer for negligence.

WC is a **social contract**:

- The employee receives **medical care and wage replacement** without proving fault.
- The employer receives **limited liability** — employees cannot sue for pain and suffering or punitive damages (subject to exceptions).

In the US, WC is state-regulated (state statutes, state funds, private carriers, or monopolistic state funds). In the UK, the system differs: employers have a **statutory liability** under the Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 for employee injuries, but **benefits** are provided through the state social security system and common-law negligence claims — there is no no-fault WC benefit scheme as such.

## Benefits Provided (US WC)

| Benefit | Description |
|---|---|
| **Medical care** | Full medical treatment for work-related injury/illness, no limit in most states |
| **Temporary total disability (TTD)** | Wage replacement while the employee is disabled and recovering (typically 2/3 of average weekly wage, subject to state maximums) |
| **Temporary partial disability (TPD)** | Wage replacement when the employee can work in a limited capacity |
| **Permanent partial disability (PPD)** | Scheduled or unscheduled awards for permanent impairment (e.g., loss of a limb, hearing loss) |
| **Permanent total disability (PTD)** | Lifetime benefits for total and permanent disability |
| **Death benefits** | Burial and weekly benefits to dependents |
| **Vocational rehabilitation** | Retraining and return-to-work services |

## The Structure of WC Insurance (US)

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| **Monopolistic states** | The state fund is the exclusive carrier (e.g., ND, OH, WA, WV, WY) |
| **Competitive states** | Private carriers compete with state funds where they exist |
| **Assigned risk / residual market** | Employers unable to obtain voluntary coverage are assigned to a pool |
| **Self-insurance** | Large employers may self-insure with state approval |
| **Group self-insurance** | Small employers pool to self-insure in some states |
| **NCCI** | The National Council on Compensation Insurance develops rates and experience rating in most states |

## Classification and Rating

### Class Codes

Every occupation and operation is assigned an **NCCI class code** with a rate per \$100 of payroll:

- **Manual rates** — state-specific, class-specific rates.
- **Payroll audit** — actual payroll by class determined at audit.
- **Premium = (Payroll ÷ 100) × Class Rate × Experience Modifier × (1 + Ex-Mod Taxes/Assessments)**

### Experience Rating

- **Experience modification (Ex-Mod / EMR)** — compares actual losses to expected losses over a period (typically 3 policy years, excluding the most recent) and produces a modifier:
  - **Below 1.00** — better than average experience (credit).
  - **Above 1.00** — worse than average experience (debit).
- Experience rating rewards safe employers and penalizes poor loss experience.
- **Large deductibles / SIR** — employers assume risk in exchange for lower premium; claims are reimbursed to the insurer above the deductible.

### Payroll and Exposure

- WC premium is driven by **payroll by class code**, so accurate payroll reporting is essential.
- **Owners/officers** — may be included or excluded under state rules.
- **Independent contractors** — must be correctly classified (misclassification is a compliance and premium issue).
- **Subcontractors** — certificates of insurance and coverage verification required.

## Underwriting Workers Compensation

### Key Underwriting Questions

1. **What operations?** — class codes, job hazards, materials.
2. **Who are the employees?** — payroll, tenure, experience, training.
3. **What is the loss history?** — frequency, severity, types of claims.
4. **What is the safety culture?** — safety programs, return-to-work, management commitment.
5. **What is the financial stability?** — payroll accuracy, credit, premium payment history.
6. **What is the state environment?** — benefit levels, litigation culture, medical costs.

### Hazard Assessment

| Hazard | Examples | Underwriting Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical** | Manual handling, machinery, falls, vehicles | Frequency/severity of injuries |
| **Chemical** | Exposure, flammability, inhalation | Occupational disease claims |
| **Ergonomic** | Repetitive motion, lifting | Soft-tissue claims |
| **Psychosocial** | Stress, workplace conflict | Mental health claims (in some states) |
| **Organizational** | Safety program weakness, training gaps | General frequency |

### Underwriting Data

| Data | Source |
|---|---|
| **Application** | Class codes, payroll, ownership, operations |
| **Loss runs** | 3–5 years WC loss history |
| **Experience mod** | NCCI/state rating bureau |
| **Safety program** | Documentation, inspections, training records |
| **Financial statements** | Payroll verification, credit |
| **Prior carrier** | Coverage verification, claims handling performance |
| **Drug-free workplace** | Testing program (discounts in many states) |
| **Return-to-work program** | Documented modified duty program |

### Underwriting Red Flags

- High frequency of minor injuries (indicative of poor safety).
- Severity spikes (large indemnity or permanent disability awards).
- Misclassification of employees or payroll.
- Poor safety documentation or no safety program.
- High turnover, seasonal employees, or inexperienced workforce.
- Claims of fraud or abuse.
- Weak financial condition of the employer.

## Controlling WC Loss

| Control | Description |
|---|---|
| **Safety programs** | Hazard identification, training, PPE |
| **Return-to-work (modified duty)** | Reduces duration of TTD |
| **Medical management** | Early intervention, choice of physician panel |
| **Claims management** | Prompt reporting, investigation, litigation control |
| **Drug-free workplace** | Testing reduces frequency |
| **Ergonomics** | Prevents soft-tissue injuries |
| **Safety culture** | Management commitment, employee engagement |

## Fraud in Workers Compensation

- **Employer fraud** — misclassification, underreporting payroll, failure to carry coverage.
- **Employee fraud** — malingering, exaggeration, off-work activities.
- **Provider fraud** — unnecessary treatment, billing scams.
- **SIU** — special investigation units investigate red flags: Monday-morning injuries, weekend accidents, inconsistent medical narratives.

## UK Employers' Liability (EL)

The UK system:

- **Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969** — every employer must have EL insurance for employee injuries/diseases (civil liability).
- **State benefits** — statutory sick pay, industrial injuries disablement benefit, and NHS treatment are funded by the state.
- **Common law claims** — employees can sue employers for negligence; EL insurance funds awards and defense.
- **EL rating** — based on payroll, occupation (class), and claims history; **excesses are generally prohibited or limited** for EL.
- **FCA conduct** — EL is regulated; renewals require disclosure of claims history.

## Summary

- WC is a **statutory, no-fault** system providing medical and wage benefits.
- Premium is driven by **class codes, payroll, and experience modification**.
- Underwriting focuses on **hazards, loss history, safety culture, financial stability, and state environment**.
- **Return-to-work, medical management, and claims handling** control WC severity.
- **UK EL** is a civil-liability system, not a no-fault benefit scheme.
