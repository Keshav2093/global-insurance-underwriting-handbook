# 6.5 Cancellations & Non-Renewals

## Overview

**Cancellation** terminates a policy before its expiration. **Non-renewal** allows the policy to expire without renewal. Both are highly regulated: insurers must follow statutory grounds, notice periods, and refund calculations, while insureds typically retain broader rights.

## Cancellation Basics

| Party | Grounds |
|---|---|
| **Insurer** | Statutory grounds only: non-payment, fraud/material misrepresentation, substantial change in risk, license suspension, catastrophic exposure, loss of reinsurance |
| **Insured** | Any reason; written notice; may be subject to short-rate penalty |

## Insurer Cancellation Grounds

| Ground | Example |
|---|---|
| **Non-payment of premium** | Missed installment; covered if policy terms met |
| **Material misrepresentation** | False application answers; discovered after binding |
| **Fraud** | Fraudulent claim or application |
| **Substantial change in risk** | Physical change increasing hazard beyond underwriting intent |
| **Loss of license** | Insured's professional license revoked |
| **Catastrophe exposure** | Multi-peril crop, catastrophe concentration |
| **Reinsurance termination** | Underlying capacity withdrawn |

Grounds vary by **state law and line of business**. Some lines (e.g., personal auto, workers comp) have strict statutory restrictions.

## Notice Requirements

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Insurer cancellation** | Statutory notice: typically 10–60 days |
| **Non-payment** | Shorter notice allowed (often 10 days) |
| **Non-renewal** | Typically 30–60 days before expiry |
| **Insured cancellation** | Usually written request; effective on receipt |
| **Notice form** | Written, mailed; some states allow electronic |

Notices must state the **effective date**, **reason** (where required), and often the **right to appeal**.

## Non-Renewal

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **Meaning** | Policy not continued at expiry |
| **Timing** | Notice within statutory period before expiry |
| **Reasons** | Often need not be stated; some states require reasons |
| **Mid-term equivalent** | Non-renewal is not a cancellation; no mid-term refund |
| **Exceptions** | Some states bar non-renewal after a claim; others allow |

Non-renewal is commonly used to manage **accumulation** or **deteriorating risk** without asserting a cancellation ground.

## Refund Calculations

| Scenario | Refund |
|---|---|
| **Insurer cancellation** | **Pro-rata** refund for unearned premium |
| **Insured cancellation (P&C)** | **Short-rate** — refund less penalty (often 10%) |
| **Non-renewal** | No refund of unearned premium (premium earned to expiry) |
| **Audit premium** | Adjusted at audit regardless of cancellation |

Short-rate penalties must comply with filed rules.

## The Process

```
1. Identify grounds and verify eligibility
2. Prepare notice (reason if required, effective date, refund computation)
3. Send notice within statutory window
4. Handle appeal/inquiry period
5. Process refund or additional premium
6. Update systems; notify producers and reinsurers
7. Document the file
```

## Compliance Considerations

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| **Late notice** | Calendar controls; statutory deadline tracking |
| **Wrong grounds** | Legal review before cancellation |
| **Unfair discrimination** | Treat similar risks consistently |
| **Notice not received** | Proof of mailing; mail-return procedures |
| **Retaliation** | No non-renewal for a single claim where barred |
| **Refund error** | Validate pro-rata/short-rate calculations |

## Summary

- **Cancellation** terminates early; **non-renewal** lets it lapse.
- Insurer grounds are **statutory and limited**.
- **Notice timing and content** are strictly regulated.
- **Refunds** differ by canceling party (pro-rata vs. short-rate).
- **Documentation and compliance controls** prevent disputes.
