# 6.4 Renewals

## Overview

**Renewal** is the continuation of coverage for a new policy period. It is the point at which the insurer **re-underwrites the risk**, re-rates the premium, and either renews, non-renews, or restructures the policy. Renewal is also a key **retention** event.

## The Renewal Cycle

```
Pre-renewal review → Offer → Re-underwriting → Rate → Issue → Deliver → Retain
```

| Stage | Activity |
|---|---|
| **Pre-renewal review** | Claims, changes, loss runs, market check |
| **Re-underwriting** | Current exposures, risk improvement, guidelines |
| **Rating** | Updated rate level, exposure base, credits/debits |
| **Offer** | Renewal terms to producer/insured |
| **Issue** | Renewal documents before expiry |
| **Retain** | Ensure acceptance; avoid coverage gaps |

## Key Renewal Inputs

| Input | Use |
|---|---|
| **Claims history** | Loss ratio, frequency, severity, large losses |
| **Current policy data** | Limits, forms, exposures |
| **Risk changes** | New operations, locations, values |
| **Underwriting guidelines** | Appetite, capacity, current stance |
| **Rate changes** | Approved rate level and classification |
| **Market conditions** | Pricing pressure, capacity |
| **Inspection updates** | New engineering or survey reports |

## Re-Underwriting the Renewal

The underwriter decides:

| Decision | Basis |
|---|---|
| **Renew as is** | Risk within appetite; loss experience acceptable |
| **Renew with changes** | Conditions, warranties, rates, deductibles |
| **Renew with restrictions** | Limit, coverage, or territory changes |
| **Non-renew** | Deterioration, claims, outside appetite |

### Renewal Underwriting Checklist

- [ ] Current loss runs and trend
- [ ] Exposure changes (payroll, receipts, values, vehicles)
- [ ] Compliance with previous warranties/conditions
- [ ] Inspections and engineering recommendations
- [ ] Financial health of the insured
- [ ] Coverage adequacy (limits vs. exposures)
- [ ] Rate adequacy vs. current data

## Rating the Renewal

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Rate level change** | Filed rate movement |
| **Experience adjustment** | E-mod, schedule, retro changes |
| **Exposure change** | Updated payroll, receipts, values |
| **Premium change compared to prior** | Retention conversation |

The renewal premium should be **explained** — rate × exposure changes and any underwriting adjustments.

## Renewal Documentation and Timing

| Requirement | Typical Rule |
|---|---|
| **Notice of renewal** | Sent before expiry (e.g., 30–60 days) |
| **Renewal policy issued** | On or before effective date |
| **Non-renewal notice** | Statutory notice period (varies by line/state) |
| **New dec page** | Updated limits, premium, forms |

## Retention and Lapse Prevention

- **Retention** — keep good business through competitive, accurate renewal pricing.
- **Lapse** — coverage ends without renewal; may require reinstatement or new application.
- **Grace period** — auto-renewal terms or late payment options.
- **Renewal contact** — reminders, quotes, service.

## Loss Ratio and Profitability at Renewal

Renewal decisions balance:

| View | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **Individual account** | Is this account profitable? |
| **Portfolio** | Does the class remain in appetite? |
| **Market** | Is pricing competitive or is the account over/under-priced? |
| **Long-term relationship** | Does the account have growth or claims potential? |

## Non-Renewal

- Must follow **statutory notice periods** and reasons.
- Producers and insured must be notified in writing.
- Evidence of alternative coverage may be required.
- Non-renewal differs from cancellation (expiry vs. mid-term).

See [6.5 Cancellations & Non-renewals](05-cancellations-nonrenewals.md).

## Summary

- Renewal is a **re-underwriting and re-rating** event.
- Use **claims, exposures, inspections, and guidelines** to decide terms.
- **Timing and notices** are regulatory and contractual.
- **Retention and lapse prevention** keep the book stable.
- Renewal decisions balance **account, portfolio, and market**.
