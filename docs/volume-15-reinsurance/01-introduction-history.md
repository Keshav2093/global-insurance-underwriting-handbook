## Executive Summary

Reinsurance is insurance for insurers. A primary (direct) insurer cedes part of its risk to a reinsurer in exchange for a premium, protecting its balance sheet against individual large losses and accumulations. Reinsurance supports capacity, stabilises loss experience, improves solvency, and enables insurers to write risks they could not otherwise retain. This chapter introduces the concepts, market participants, and historical development of reinsurance.

---

## 15.1.1 What Is Reinsurance?

**Reinsurance** is a contract under which one insurer (the **ceding company** or **cedent**) transfers all or part of a risk or portfolio of risks to another insurer (the **reinsurer**) in exchange for a premium.

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Ceding company / cedent** | The primary insurer that transfers risk |
| **Reinsurer** | The insurer that assumes the transferred risk |
| **Cession** | The amount of risk transferred to the reinsurer |
| **Retention / net line** | The amount of risk the ceding company keeps |
| **Retrocession** | Reinsurance of a reinsurer's liabilities |
| **Retrocessionaire** | The reinsurer that accepts retrocession |

### Purpose of Reinsurance

| Function | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Capacity** | Enable writing larger individual risks than net resources allow |
| **Catastrophe protection** | Protect against accumulations of loss (windstorm, earthquake, flood) |
| **Stabilisation** | Smooth loss experience between good and bad years |
| **Solvency** | Reduce required capital and improve solvency ratios |
| **Capital management** | Release capital for growth or return to shareholders |
| **Expertise** | Access market knowledge, pricing, and risk engineering |
| **Portfolio management** | Manage exposure concentration by line, class, or territory |
| **New venture support** | Support entry into new markets and products |

## 15.1.2 How Reinsurance Works

### Proportional Flow

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[Policyholder] -->|Premium| B[Primary Insurer]
    B -->|Ceded premium| C[Reinsurer]
    C -->|Share of losses| B
    B -->|Claims payment| A
```

### Contract Relationship

| Element | Primary Contract | Reinsurance Contract |
|---------|------------------|----------------------|
| Parties | Policyholder & insurer | Ceding company & reinsurer |
| Subject | Risk (property/person) | Liabilities of the ceding company |
| Premium | Policy premium | Ceded premium / reinsurance premium |
| Duration | Policy term | Treaty term (often 12 months) |
| Applicable law | Policy contract | Reinsurance contract |
| Claim | Policyholder claim | Recovery from reinsurer |

## 15.1.3 Key Market Participants

| Participant | Role |
|-------------|------|
| **Primary insurers** | Write direct risks; purchase reinsurance |
| **Professional reinsurers** | Dedicated reinsurance companies (e.g., Munich Re, Swiss Re) |
| **Reinsurance brokers** | Intermediaries who place reinsurance (e.g., Guy Carpenter, Aon Re, Gallagher Re) |
| **Lloyd's of London** | Market where syndicates underwrite reinsurance |
| **Specialist carriers** | Insurance groups with reinsurance divisions |
| **Retrocessionaires** | Accept risk from other reinsurers |
| **Capital market participants** | ILS, catastrophe bonds, industry loss warranties |
| **Regulators** | Supervise solvency and reinsurance transactions |
| **Rating agencies** | Assess reinsurer security and claims-paying ability |

### Reinsurer Security

| Consideration | Why It Matters |
|---------------|----------------|
| **Credit rating** | A.M. Best, S&P, Moody's, Fitch ratings of the reinsurer |
| **Collateral** | Trust funds for alien reinsurers writing in the US |
| **Cedent credit risk** | Reinsurer may fail to pay recoveries |
| **Regulatory recognition** | State/territory approval of credit for reinsurance |

## 15.1.4 History of Reinsurance

| Era | Development |
|-----|-------------|
| **14th century** | Marine insurance emerges in Italian city-states |
| **c. 1347** | Earliest insurance contracts issued; reinsurance-like agreements appear |
| **17th century** | Lloyd's Coffee House (1688) — Lloyd's of London develops |
| **1800s** | Modern reinsurance companies form: Kölnische Rück (1846), Swiss Re (1863), Munich Re (1880) |
| **1906** | San Francisco earthquake — major test of reinsurance market |
| **1900s** | Treaty reinsurance evolves; proportional structures dominate |
| **1950s–1970s** | Excess of loss treaties increase in popularity |
| **1980s** | Financial reinsurance and alternative structures emerge |
| **1990s** | Catastrophe bonds and insurance-linked securities (ILS) develop |
| **2000s–2010s** | Convergence of capital markets and reinsurance; sidecars, ILWs |
| **2020s** | Data analytics, AI-driven underwriting, cyber reinsurance growth |

### 1906 San Francisco Earthquake

> **Example:** The 1906 earthquake and subsequent fire caused insured losses representing a significant fraction of US insurers' capital at the time. Several insurers defaulted, demonstrating the importance of reinsurance capacity and leading to more structured catastrophe protection.

## 15.1.5 Why Reinsurance Matters

### For Individual Risks

| Benefit | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Larger limits | A $100M airline risk with $20M net capacity requires $80M reinsurance |
| Specialised perils | Nuclear, aviation, satellite |
| Facultative placement | Single large risk placed case by case |

### For Catastrophes

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Catastrophe XL** | Excess of loss inure to a single event |
| **Aggregation** | Protection across a portfolio |
| **Non-proportional** | Reinsurer pays above an attachment point |
| **Modelling** | Cat models assess probable maximum loss (PML) |

### For Solvency & Capital

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Risk-based capital** | Reinsurance reduces required capital |
| **Solvency II / regulatory capital** | Credit for reinsurance under solvency frameworks |
| **Earnings stability** | Protect against volatile experience |
| **Recovery of losses** | Reinsurer pays its share of claims |

---

## 15.1.6 Insurance vs Reinsurance

| Aspect | Insurance | Reinsurance |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Contracting parties | Policyholder and insurer | Insurer and reinsurer |
| Subject matter | Original risk | Insurer's liability on original risk |
| Insurable interest | Policyholder's interest | Cedent's exposure |
| Regulation | Extensive consumer protection | Wholesale, less consumer protection |
| Claimant | Policyholder | Ceding company |
| Distribution | Agents, brokers, direct | Reinsurance brokers, direct |

## Key Takeaways

1. **Reinsurance is insurance for insurers** — it transfers risk from a primary insurer to a reinsurer.
2. **It serves capacity, catastrophe protection, stabilisation, and capital functions.**
3. **The market includes professional reinsurers, brokers, Lloyd's, and capital markets.**
4. **Historical catastrophes shaped modern reinsurance structures.**
