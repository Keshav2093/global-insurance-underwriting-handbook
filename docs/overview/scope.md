# Handbook Scope

This handbook defines the subject matter, boundaries, and intended audience of the Global Insurance Underwriting Handbook.

## Subject Matter

The handbook covers **non-life (property-casualty / general) insurance** in depth, with essential coverage of **life and health** for completeness. The core focus is the **underwriting function** — the evaluation, selection, classification, and pricing of risk — together with the operational functions that surround it: policy administration, rating, claims, and regulation.

### In Scope

- Insurance fundamentals and the principles of insurance (insurable interest, utmost good faith, indemnity, subrogation, contribution, proximate cause).
- Personal lines: private passenger auto, homeowners, dwelling fire, personal umbrella, life and health essentials.
- Commercial lines: commercial property, general liability, businessowners package, commercial auto, workers compensation, professional liability, ocean/inland marine, cyber, D&O/management liability, surety and fidelity, and specialty lines.
- The underwriting process: submission intake, risk inspection, evaluation, selection, pricing, documentation, and file management.
- Rating and pricing: rate making, manual/class rating, experience and schedule rating, loss ratios, and profitability measurement.
- Policy administration: documents, issuance, endorsements, renewals, premium transactions, and transactional accuracy.
- Claims: first notice of loss, investigation, evaluation, subrogation, and fraud/SIU.
- US and UK regulatory environments, market conduct, and compliance.

### Out of Scope

- Detailed actuarial reserving science (loss reserving is referenced but not developed as a full actuarial discipline).
- Investment management of insurer portfolios.
- Reinsurance contract drafting (reinsurance principles are summarized where they affect underwriting capacity).
- Jurisdiction-specific rate forms and filings to the level of individual state/country manuals (state snapshots are illustrative).
- Legal advice or regulatory interpretation for specific cases.

## Jurisdictional Coverage

The handbook addresses two primary markets with distinct regulatory and market structures:

- **United States** — state-based regulation, admitted vs. surplus lines, NAIC involvement, and market conduct.
- **United Kingdom** — FCA and PRA dual regulation, Solvency II, and conduct of business rules.

Where the two markets differ materially (e.g., "reasonable and prudent person" vs. "average consumer", tort structure, auto no-fault states), the differences are called out explicitly.

## Audience

The handbook is written for:

- **New and trainee underwriters** seeking a structured introduction.
- **Experienced underwriters** needing a reference across lines and markets.
- **Pricing analysts and actuaries** wanting the operational context of rating.
- **Claims professionals** needing the underwriting perspective on loss.
- **Regulators, compliance officers, trainers, and assessors** using the material for review and assessment.

## Depth and Style

- Each chapter is **self-contained** but cross-linked to related chapters.
- **Coursework files** (checklists, proposal forms, guidelines) support applied learning.
- Definitions are **plain-English first**, then technical where relevant.
- Data and examples are illustrative, not authoritative rate information.

## Currency and Maintenance

Insurance products, forms, and regulations change continuously. Readers must verify current forms, rates, and rules with the appropriate insurer, rate filing, and regulator. This handbook is periodically revised; check the repository for update history.
