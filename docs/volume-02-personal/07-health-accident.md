# 2.7 Health & Accident Insurance

## Overview

**Health and accident insurance** protects individuals against the financial consequences of illness, injury, and disability. This chapter covers the personal health and accident products an underwriter encounters:

- **Medical expense insurance** — hospital, surgical, and physician coverage.
- **Disability income insurance** — replaces lost income during disability.
- **Critical illness insurance** — lump-sum payment on diagnosis of specified conditions.
- **Accident insurance** — pays benefits for accidental injury (medical, lump-sum, AD&D).
- **Long-term care insurance** — covers custodial and nursing care needs.

Health insurance is heavily regulated and differs fundamentally between the US and UK markets.

## Medical Expense Insurance

### US Market

- **Private health insurance** — employer-sponsored group plans and individual/family plans.
- **ACA (Affordable Care Act)** marketplaces — guaranteed issue (no medical underwriting for most plans), essential health benefits, premium subsidies.
- **Medicare** — federal coverage for those 65+ and certain disabilities; **Medigap** supplemental; **Medicare Advantage** (private plans).
- **Medicaid** — state/federal program for low-income individuals.
- **Catastrophic plans** — young adults, high deductible.
- **HSAs (Health Savings Accounts)** — tax-advantaged accounts paired with high-deductible plans.

### UK Market

- **Private medical insurance (PMI)** — covers private hospital treatment for acute conditions; not emergencies or chronic conditions usually.
- Underwritten individually or by employer groups; **medical underwriting** (full, moratorium, or moratorium with exclusions) applies.
- **NHS** is the primary public system; PMI supplements access.

### Medical Underwriting

- **Individual medical** — questionnaires, medical records, lab tests for higher sums.
- **Guaranteed issue** (group) — no individual underwriting; group risk rated.
- **Moratorium underwriting** (individual PMI) — pre-existing conditions excluded for a period (e.g., 5 years) if symptom-free and untreated.
- **Underwriting factors:** age, health history, family history, smoking, occupation, lifestyle, existing conditions.

## Disability Insurance

### Short-Term vs. Long-Term

| Feature | Short-Term Disability (STD) | Long-Term Disability (LTD) |
|---|---|---|
| Elimination period | 0–14 days | 90–180 days (or longer) |
| Benefit period | Up to 6 months–2 years | To age 65 or retirement |
| Benefit amount | 60–70% of income | 50–65% of income |
| Common in | Employer group plans | Employer and individual plans |

### Individual Disability (IDI)

- **Own occupation** — disability defined against own occupation (stronger) vs. **any occupation** (weaker).
- **Residual/partial disability** — benefits for reduced income.
- **Cost of living adjustment (COLA)** — inflation protection.
- **Non-cancellable** — insurer cannot cancel or raise rates; valuable and more expensive.
- **Guaranteed renewable** — insurer cannot cancel but may adjust rates by class.

### Underwriting Factors

- **Age and gender** — morbidity curves.
- **Occupation and duty class** — sedentary vs. manual; risk of injury.
- **Income** — benefit limit relative to earned income (replacement ratio cap).
- **Health history** — musculoskeletal, mental health, cardiovascular claims.
- **Lifestyle** — smoking, alcohol, extreme sports.
- **Financial motivation** — benefit must not exceed income (moral hazard control).

## Critical Illness Insurance

- Pays a **lump sum** on diagnosis of a defined list of conditions (cancer, heart attack, stroke, etc.).
- Underwriting similar to life insurance (medical questionnaire, exam for higher sums).
- Age limits (e.g., up to 70), benefit periods, and definitions vary by product.
- **Value** — covers recovery costs, income loss, or debt.
- **Add-on** — often attached to life or mortgage products.

## Accident Insurance

- **Accidental Death & Dismemberment (AD&D)** — pays on accidental death or specified injuries (loss of limb, sight).
- **Accident medical** — pays medical expenses from an accident.
- **Accident income** — short-term weekly benefits during accident recovery.
- **Underwriting** — light; occupation and hazardous avocations are the main factors.
- Often **guaranteed issue** or simplified issue; excludes illness conditions.

## Long-Term Care (LTC) Insurance

- Covers nursing home, assisted living, and home care costs.
- **Triggers** — inability to perform activities of daily living (ADLs: bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, transferring, continence) or cognitive impairment.
- **Underwriting** — extensive medical underwriting; cognitive and functional screening; age at issue.
- **Hybrid products** — LTC riders on life insurance or annuities (asset-based LTC).
- **US** — private LTC market, state partnership programs; **UK** — care needs often met by social care funding; private "care insurance" less common (policies like annuities covering care costs in some forms).

## Regulation and Considerations

| Market | Key Regulations |
|---|---|
| **US** | ACA (guaranteed issue, essential benefits), HIPAA (portability, privacy), ERISA (employer plans), state insurance regulation |
| **UK** | FCA consumer duty, fair value, advised sales for protection; medical insurance conduct rules |

Key considerations for underwriters:

- **Moral hazard** — keep replacement ratios low for disability; verify income.
- **Anti-selection** — guaranteed issue products require strong group governance.
- **Definitions drive claims** — "own occupation" vs. "any occupation" changes claims experience significantly.
- **Chronic vs. acute** — PMI excludes chronic conditions; LTC covers chronic needs.
- **Data** — morbidity tables, claims studies, and reinsurer guidance underpin pricing.

## Summary

- Health and accident products include **medical, disability, critical illness, accident, and long-term care**.
- **Medical underwriting** ranges from guaranteed issue to full individual underwriting.
- **Disability** underwriting focuses on occupation, income, and health.
- **Definitions, benefit periods, and triggers** are the principal claims drivers.
- Regulation (ACA, FCA) constrains underwriting and pricing.
