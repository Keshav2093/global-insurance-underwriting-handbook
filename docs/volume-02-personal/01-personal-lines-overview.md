# 2.1 Personal Lines Overview

## What Are Personal Lines?

**Personal lines** are insurance products sold to individuals and families for their personal, non-business exposures. The principal products are:

- **Personal auto** — vehicles owned and driven by individuals.
- **Homeowners** — owner-occupied homes, which combine property and liability coverages.
- **Dwelling fire & other personal property** — non-owner-occupied dwellings (e.g., rental properties), tenant contents, condominium units, mobile homes, and personal property floaters.
- **Personal umbrella / excess liability** — high-limit liability above underlying policies.
- **Life and health** — term and permanent life, medical, disability, critical illness, and accident products (covered in Volume 2 for personal insurance completeness).

Personal lines are typically **high-volume, low-premium, standardized** products. Their economics depend on:

- **Large, homogeneous books** across which the law of large numbers operates.
- **Efficient, automated underwriting and rating** (straight-through processing).
- **Low unit acquisition and service costs**.
- **Accurate pricing** across thousands of distinct rating cells.

## The Personal Lines Customer

| Segment | Characteristics | Product Needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Young single** | First car, renters | Auto, renter contents |
| **Young family** | Home purchase, children | Auto ×2+, homeowners, umbrella |
| **Established family** | Multiple vehicles, home, assets | Full package, umbrella |
| **Empty nester** | Reduced risks, travel | Auto, home, umbrella, travel |
| **Retiree** | Low mileage, fixed income | Auto, home, umbrella |

Customers buy personal lines through:

- **Independent agents** — shop across insurers; advise on coverage.
- **Captive agents** — one insurer's products.
- **Direct** — online, phone, apps.
- **Bancassurance and affinity** — banks, employers, clubs.

## Product Structure

### Auto

The **Personal Auto Policy (PAP)** provides:

- **Liability coverage** (bodily injury and property damage) for the insured's legal liability arising from auto accidents.
- **Medical payments / personal injury protection (PIP)**.
- **Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM)** coverage.
- **Physical damage** — collision and other-than-collision (comprehensive).

### Homeowners

Homeowners forms (HO-3, HO-5, HO-8 and modified forms) combine:

- **Property coverage** — dwelling, other structures, personal property, loss of use.
- **Liability coverage** — personal liability and medical payments to others.
- Often with inflation guard, replacement cost, and scheduled personal property endorsements.

### Personal Umbrella

Provides **excess liability** (typically \$1M+) above the insured's underlying auto and home liability limits, plus some drop-down coverages for claims not covered below.

## Distribution and Commission Economics

| Channel | Commission/Margin | Implications |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | Low acquisition cost | Price-led; strong data capture |
| Captive agent | Moderate | Brand loyalty; service quality |
| Independent agent | Higher | Multiple insurers compete for the account |
| MGA/binder | Varied | Specialty or niche programs |

Commission rates are regulated in some markets (e.g., UK rules on disclosure and conflicts in the past) and shape the overall expense ratio.

## Personal Lines Rating Basics

Personal lines are **class-rated** — the premium is determined from rating tables by a set of classification factors:

| Line | Typical Rating Factors |
|---|---|
| Auto | Territory (where garaged), driver age, driving record (MVR points), miles driven, vehicle make/model, prior insurance, credit-based insurance score (in many states) |
| Home | Territory, construction (frame vs. masonry), protection class, age and condition, replacement cost, deductible, claims history, credit |
| Umbrella | Underlying policy limits and quality, vehicles/drivers, home value, watercraft |

Personal lines rate filings must comply with state insurance law in the US (rate filings, prior approval/file-and-use) and with FCA rules in the UK (fair value, price walking measures limiting renewal pricing differentials, 2018-19 rules).

## Underwriting Personal Lines

Personal lines underwriting is largely automated but the "manual" skill remains:

1. **Eligibility** — product, territory, credit/prior insurance.
2. **Risk assessment** — MVR, loss runs, CLUE report, inspection if needed.
3. **Pricing** — rating engine output; override authority limited.
4. **Terms** — deductibles, endorsements, coverage limits.
5. **Documentation and compliance** — state/conduct rules: adverse action notices, privacy, fair discrimination.

Key personal lines underwriting tools:

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| **MVR (Motor Vehicle Report)** | Driving record, license status |
| **CLUE (Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange)** | Property and auto claims history |
| **ISO A-Plus / claim databases** | Prior loss history |
| **Credit-based insurance score** | Predicts loss propensity (subject to state restrictions) |
| **Insurance score / prior insurance** | Lapse and gap indicators |
| **Inspections** | Verify condition, characteristics, and exposures |

## Regulation and Fairness

Personal lines are tightly regulated because they involve consumers:

- **Rate regulation** — rates must be adequate, not excessive, and not unfairly discriminatory (US); fair value and value measures (UK).
- **Form standards** — plain language, restrictions on exclusions.
- **Market conduct** — claims handling, complaints, advertising.
- **Adverse action notices** — required when underwriting declines or adverse decisions are based on credit reports or MVRs.
- **Privacy** — collecting and using personal data requires compliance (e.g., GLBA in the US; UK GDPR).

## Trends in Personal Lines

- **Telematics (usage-based insurance)** — devices and apps reward safe driving.
- **Smart home/IoT** — leak and fire sensors inform pricing and loss mitigation.
- **AI and machine learning** — pricing models, fraud detection, chatbots.
- **Embedded insurance** — buying cover at point of sale (e.g., at a dealership or travel booking).
- **Pricing transparency and unfair discrimination rules** — regulators increasingly scrutinize the use of personal data (e.g., UK "price walking" rules and proposals to address "fair value").

## Implications for Underwriters

- Personal lines underwriters manage **exceptions** at the margins: unusual risks, high-value homes, high-risk drivers, new products.
- The core discipline is **data quality, accurate rating, and consistent policy application**.
- Underwriters must understand the **rating engine and its rules** to price exceptions correctly.
- **Portfolio management** requires monitoring conversions, retention, loss ratios by territory/class, and regulatory exposure.

## Summary

- Personal lines are **standardized, high-volume, class-rated** products.
- The five building blocks: **auto, home, dwelling fire/other property, umbrella, life & health**.
- Underwriting is mostly automated; underwriters handle exceptions and governance.
- **Regulation** focuses on rate fairness, claims handling, privacy, and adverse action notices.
