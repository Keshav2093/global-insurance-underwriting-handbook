# 2.4 Dwelling Fire & Other Personal Property

## Overview

Not all residential property is owner-occupied under a homeowners policy. **Dwelling fire** and related coverages serve:

- **Non-owner-occupied dwellings** — rental properties, vacant dwellings, seasonal homes.
- **Owner-occupied dwellings that do not qualify for HO** — older homes, unique construction, high-value, or where the owner prefers a simpler form.
- **Tenants** — renters contents and liability.
- **Condominium unit owners** — unit contents and improvements.
- **Mobile/manufactured homes** — where HO-7 or a specialist policy applies.
- **Unique personal property** — scheduled jewelry, fine arts, collectibles, watercraft.

## The Dwelling Fire (DF) Policy

The **Dwelling Fire (DP)** program (ISO DP-1, DP-2, DP-3) is the standard US form:

| Form | Peril Basis | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|
| **DP-1 (Basic)** | Named perils (fire, lightning, internal explosion) | Low-cost; vacant or seasonal dwellings |
| **DP-2 (Broad)** | 16 named perils | Rental or qualifying owner-occupied |
| **DP-3 (Special)** | Open perils for dwelling; named perils for contents | Owner-occupied or where broader cover needed |

### Key Features

- **Coverage A (Dwelling), B (Other Structures), C (Contents), D (Fair Rental Value / Loss of Use)**.
- **Valuation:** replacement cost or actual cash value options; DP-1 is often ACV.
- **Deductibles:** flat or percentage-based.
- **Rental coverage:** **fair rental value** for landlord-owned homes; contents are normally not covered for tenant property (tenant needs own policy).
- **Vacancy:** often limited or suspended after 30–60 days vacant (vandalism, water damage exclusions apply while vacant).

### Underwriting Considerations for Dwelling Fire

| Factor | Underwriting Concern |
|---|---|
| **Occupancy** | Owner-occupied, tenant-occupied, seasonal, vacant |
| **Tenant type** | Single family, multi-family, short-term rental, rooming house |
| **Condition** | Age, roof, systems, deferred maintenance (higher for rentals) |
| **Property management** | Managed vs. self-managed; screening of tenants |
| **Protection** | Fire district, hydrants, alarms, sprinklers |
| **Catastrophe exposure** | Flood, wind, wildfire zones |
| **Replacement cost adequacy** | Accurate RCE needed; underinsurance penalizes at claim time |

### Rental Property Underwriting

- **Screening** — some insurers require the landlord to use tenant screening (credit, eviction history).
- **Limits** — contents coverage for the landlord's fixtures and personal property only.
- **Loss of rent** — verify actual rental income/agreement.
- **Short-term rentals** — normally excluded or specially rated; require endorsement.
- **Multi-unit dwellings** — higher frequency; check fire separation, egress, smoke detectors.

## Tenants (Renters) Insurance

**HO-4 (Contents)** covers:

- **Personal property** (contents) against named or open perils.
- **Loss of use** (additional living expense).
- **Personal liability and medical payments** for the tenant.

Underwriting focuses on:

- **Building safety** — the landlord's building systems affect loss potential (fire, water).
- **Claims history** — tenant contents losses.
- **Pets and liability exposures**.
- **High-risk contents** — cash, jewelry, guns, watercraft (limit or schedule).

## Condominium (HO-6)

**HO-6** covers:

- **Personal property**, unit owner's additions/improvements.
- **Loss of use**, personal liability, medical payments.

The condominium association's **master policy** covers the building and common areas. The unit owner needs **walls-in coverage** for their improvements and betterments. Underwriting checks the **master policy adequacy** and the association's **financial health** (special assessments, litigation, reserve adequacy).

## Mobile and Manufactured Homes

**HO-7 / specialist mobile home policies** cover:

- The **mobile home unit** (including attached additions).
- **Contents**, liability, and loss of use (as applicable).

Underwriting considerations:

- **Age and condition** of the unit; HUD certification.
- **Tie-downs, foundation, and anchoring** — wind risk.
- **Location** — parks vs. private land; flood/wind zones.
- **Use** — permanent residence vs. seasonal.

## Other Residential Structures

| Exposure | Coverage |
|---|---|
| **Seasonal/second homes** | Dwelling fire or HO with higher rates, vacancy limitations |
| **Row/townhouses** | HO or dwelling fire; fire separation and association arrangements |
| **Farm/ranch dwellings** | Farmowners package or HO with farm endorsements |
| **Student housing / dorm contents** | Renter contents forms |
| **Accessory dwelling units (ADUs)** | Often covered under the main home policy, subject to limits and conditions |

## Personal Property Floaters and Scheduled Articles

The homeowners policy provides limited amounts for certain categories:

- Jewelry, furs: typically \$1,500–\$2,500 limit.
- Money, securities: limited (e.g., \$200–\$500).
- Watercraft: limited liability and property.

**Scheduled personal property (floater)** endorsements provide:

- **All-risk-type coverage** (open perils) for the scheduled items.
- **Agreed value** or stated amount.
- **Worldwide coverage**.
- **No deductible** options.

Articles commonly scheduled: jewelry, watches, fine arts, cameras, musical instruments, firearms, collectibles, golf equipment.

Underwriting the floater:

- Appraisal or proof of value.
- Security measures (safe, alarm).
- Risk location and travel patterns.
- Loss history (theft/fire frequency).

## Watercraft

Personal watercraft (boats, PWCs) may be covered under:

- Homeowners (small boats, limited liability and property).
- **Separate boat/yacht policy** — for larger or higher-value craft:
  - Hull coverage (agreed value or ACV).
  - Liability (P&I or broad form).
  - Medical payments.
  - Uninsured boater.

Underwriting factors: boat type, size, horsepower, age, storage (dry vs. wet), operator experience, navigable waters, and loss history.

## UK Perspective

- **Buildings & contents** as covered in [2.3](03-homeowners.md) apply to owner-occupied and rented homes.
- **Landlord insurance** — buildings cover for rental property; **contents** may include landlord's fixtures; loss of rent/rent guarantee covers; liability for the landlord's interest.
- **Tenant's insurance** — contents only; buildings are the landlord's.
- **Scheduled valuables** — "specified items" added to contents policies; high-value items require individual sums insured.

## Summary

- Dwelling fire forms serve **non-owner-occupied** and non-qualifying dwellings.
- **Renters (HO-4), condos (HO-6), and mobile homes (HO-7)** address specific occupancies.
- **Scheduled floaters** cover high-value personal property with broader terms.
- Underwriting emphasizes **occupancy, condition, protection, catastrophe exposure, and coverage adequacy**.
