# 2.2 Personal Auto Insurance

## Overview

**Personal auto insurance** covers private passenger vehicles owned or used by individuals and families. It protects against:

- **Third-party liability** — bodily injury (BI) and property damage (PD) to others for which the insured is legally liable.
- **First-party losses** — damage to the insured's own vehicle (collision, comprehensive), medical costs, and loss of use.
- **UM/UIM** — claims by the insured against uninsured or underinsured motorists.

Auto insurance is required in almost all US states (with varying minimum limits and fault systems) and is mandatory in the UK for injury to third parties. It is the largest single personal lines product by premium.

## The Personal Auto Policy (PAP)

The standard US Personal Auto Policy (ISO PP 00 01) is the reference form. It has six parts:

### Part A — Liability Coverage

- Insures **bodily injury** and **property damage** liability of the **named insured** and **family members** arising from the ownership, maintenance, or use of a covered auto.
- Covers claims and **defense costs** (defense is generally in addition to the limit in many states).
- Provides coverage for covered persons using a **non-owned auto** (e.g., a rental or borrowed car) within limits.
- **Exclusions:** intentional acts, using a vehicle without a reasonable belief of right, bodily injury to employees (covered by workers compensation), commercial use, racing, and vehicles furnished for regular use without coverage.

### Part B — Medical Payments (MedPay)

- Pays reasonable medical and funeral expenses for the insured and passengers injured in an auto accident, **regardless of fault**.
- Limited to a stated per-person amount.
- Does not duplicate health insurance benefits in all states; coordinates with those policies.

### Part C — Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM)

- **UM** — pays for the insured's bodily injury caused by an **uninsured** motorist or a hit-and-run driver.
- **UIM** — pays when the **at-fault driver's limits are insufficient** to cover the insured's damages.
- Offered or required in most states; coverage triggers and stacking rules vary by state.

### Part D — Coverage for Damage to Your Auto

- **Collision** — damage to the insured's vehicle from collision with another object or overturn.
- **Other than collision (Comprehensive)** — damage from fire, theft, vandalism, wind, hail, flood, falling objects, or animal collision.
- Each is subject to a **deductible** chosen by the insured.
- **Replacement cost / stated amount / actual cash value** options exist for newer, classic, or modified vehicles.
- **Transportation expenses** and loss of use may be added by endorsement.

### Part E — Duties After an Accident

- Prompt notice to the insurer.
- Cooperation with investigation and defense.
- Submitting to examinations under oath when required.
- Authorizing the insurer to obtain records.

### Part F — General Provisions

- Bankruptcy of the insured does not relieve the insurer.
- Cancellation and non-renewal provisions.
- Fraud, concealment, misrepresentation can void coverage.
- The policy is not automatically assignable.

## Rating Personal Auto

Auto insurance is **class-rated** using a large set of rating factors. Key factors:

| Factor | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Territory** | Where the vehicle is principally garaged; reflects traffic density, theft rates, litigation climate |
| **Operator age and gender** | Younger and less experienced drivers have higher frequency and severity (age restrictions on gender apply in some jurisdictions due to equality law) |
| **Driving record (MVR)** | Accidents, violations, points, and suspensions |
| **Vehicle use** | Pleasure vs. business vs. farm; annual mileage |
| **Vehicle characteristics** | Make, model, model year, body type, value, safety and anti-theft features |
| **Prior insurance / continuity** | Gaps in coverage correlate with higher risk (this is a pricing and underwriting factor) |
| **Credit-based insurance score** | Used in many states; legally restricted in others |
| **Claims history** | At-fault accidents and claim frequency; first-party claim surcharges |
| **Discounts** | Multi-policy, multi-vehicle, safe driver, defensive driving course, telematics, good student, anti-theft, paperless |

### Rating Example (Illustrative)

| Factor | Relativities |
|---|---|
| Base premium | \$800 |
| Territory | × 1.10 |
| Young driver | × 1.85 |
| Clean MVR | × 0.90 |
| Annual miles < 8,000 | × 0.90 |
| Anti-theft device | × 0.95 |
| **Indicated premium** | \$800 × 1.10 × 1.85 × 0.90 × 0.90 × 0.95 ≈ **\$1,256** |

*(Illustrative only — actual rates vary by insurer, state, and filing.)*

## Underwriting Personal Auto

### Eligibility Rules

- Vehicle type — private passenger autos; some insurers accept light pickups/SUVs.
- Number of vehicles and drivers.
- Territory — some insurers do not write in high-risk territories.
- Business use — must be classified or declined.

### Underwriting Data Sources

| Source | Use |
|---|---|
| **Application** | Vehicle, driver, use, prior insurance, garage address |
| **MVR** | License status, violations, accidents |
| **CLUE / A-Plus** | Personal auto loss history (5 years) |
| **ISO / claim databases** | Loss runs across carriers |
| **Credit-based score** | Insurance score (subject to state law) |
| **Telematics** | Actual driving behavior when installed |
| **Prior insurer verification** | Continuous coverage |

### Driver Classification

Every licensed household member who drives is classified. Household drivers include:

- Named insured and spouse.
- Other relatives living in the household (licensed).
- Drivers with regular use of a vehicle.

Sometimes a driver must be **excluded** (signed driver exclusion) to obtain coverage — e.g., a high-risk teenager or an unlicensed individual. Exclusions are strictly interpreted; a claims denial can result if an excluded driver uses the vehicle without permission being established.

### Underwriting Declines

Common declines in personal auto:

- Major violations: DUI/DWI (within a defined period), reckless driving, license suspension.
- Multiple at-fault accidents in a stated period.
- Fraud or material misrepresentation.
- Non-payment of premium history.
- Use of vehicle in a business not rated (e.g., delivery).
- High-performance or modified vehicles outside appetite.

### Telematics and Usage-Based Insurance (UBI)

- **Pay-as-you-drive (PAYD)** — premium based on miles driven.
- **Pay-how-you-drive (PHYD)** — premium based on driving behavior: speed, hard braking, cornering, time of day, phone use.
- Telematics improves risk selection, gives safe drivers discounts, and provides claim data (e.g., crash reconstruction).

## No-Fault and Fault Systems

| System | Description |
|---|---|
| **Tort (fault)** | The at-fault driver's insurer pays third-party BI/PD claims; the injured party may sue for damages |
| **No-fault** | Each driver's own insurer pays their medical and economic losses up to a threshold; tort rights limited (e.g., NY, FL, MA, MI historically) |
| **Choice ("add-on")** | Tort system with optional first-party benefits (e.g., "no-fault add-on" states) |

State no-fault laws affect:

- **PIP coverages and limits** — required first-party benefits.
- **Thresholds** — verbal vs. monetary thresholds for suing.
- **Claim severities** — litigation rates and BI costs.

Underwriters must know the **state system** where the vehicle is garaged.

## UK Personal Auto (Motor) Insurance

UK private car insurance operates under:

- **Road Traffic Act 1988** — third-party bodily injury compulsory cover.
- **FCA regulation** — fair value, pricing rules (including the "price walking" ban for renewals), and disclosure.
- Cover levels: **Third Party Only**, **Third Party Fire & Theft**, and **Comprehensive**.
- Rating factors similar to the US: age, driving experience, no-claims discount (NCD), vehicle group, territory (postcode), and occupation.
- **No claims discount (NCD)** — up to a maximum (e.g., 9+ years) reduces premium; protected NCD available.
- **Telematics** ("black box") policies are common for young drivers.

## Claims Considerations

- **FNOL** — capture accident details, injuries, witnesses.
- **Coverage determination** — verify covered auto, driver, use.
- **Liability investigation** — police reports, statements, scene evidence.
- **Physical damage appraisal** — repair vs. total loss; ACV vs. replacement cost.
- **Total loss** — vehicle value using market comparisons; salvage handling.
- **Subrogation** — pursue at-fault third parties (in tort states).
- **Fraud** — staged accidents, inflated claims, "slip-and-fall" style auto injury fraud, SIU referral.

## Summary

- Personal auto provides **liability, medical, UM/UIM, and physical damage** coverages.
- Rating is **class-based** on territory, driver, use, vehicle, and experience factors.
- Underwriting relies on **MVR, claim history, credit (where allowed), and telematics**.
- State/legal systems (tort vs. no-fault) and UK motor rules shape both coverage and pricing.
