# 2.6 Life Insurance

## Overview

**Life insurance** pays a defined sum of money (the **sum assured**) to a named **beneficiary** upon the death of the **life assured**, in exchange for premiums. Unlike property and casualty insurance, life insurance is:

- **Not a contract of indemnity** — a human life cannot be valued; the sum assured is fixed in the policy.
- **A long-term contract** — usually written for the whole of life or a term of years.
- **Valuation-dependent** — underwriting mortality risk (and morbidity for related products).

Life insurance serves:

- **Income replacement** — protecting dependents against loss of the breadwinner's income.
- **Debt protection** — covering mortgages, loans, and business debts.
- **Estate planning** — liquidity for inheritance tax, wealth transfer.
- **Business protection** — key person, shareholder buy-sell funding.
- **Savings and investment** — whole life and endowment products build cash value.

## Types of Life Insurance

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Term insurance** | Provides cover for a fixed term (e.g., 10, 20, 30 years). Pays only if death occurs within the term. No cash value. |
| **Whole life** | Permanent cover for the whole of life; builds cash value; premium level. |
| **Endowment** | Pays the sum assured at the end of the term or on earlier death. Combines protection with savings. |
| **Universal life** | Flexible premium permanent cover; cash value earns interest; adjustable death benefit. |
| **Variable/unit-linked** | Premiums invested in funds; cash value and death benefit may vary with investment performance. |
| **Indexed universal life** | Cash value linked to a market index. |
| **Joint life** | Covers two lives (often spouses) — pays on first or second death depending on form. |
| **Credit life** | Covers a debt (mortgage, loan) — pays the balance on death. |
| **Group life** | Employer-sponsored cover for employees, usually term-based. |

## Underwriting Life Insurance

### Underwriting Questions

1. **Mortality risk** — will the life assured die during the policy term?
2. **Morbidity risk** (for riders) — will the life assure become disabled or critically ill?
3. **Financial need** — the sum assured must be justifiable (insurable interest + needs).
4. **Moral hazard** — could the policyholder benefit from the death (suicide, homicide)?

### Key Underwriting Factors

| Factor | Impact |
|---|---|
| **Age** | Higher mortality with age |
| **Gender** | Female mortality generally lower than male at most ages |
| **Smoking/tobacco use** | Significant mortality factor; smokers priced higher |
| **Health history** | Conditions (heart, cancer, diabetes, respiratory) drive underwriting and rate class |
| **Family history** | Hereditary conditions (heart disease, cancer) |
| **Occupation** | Hazardous occupations (aviation, mining, deep-sea) |
| **Lifestyle/avocation** | Extreme sports, sky-diving, scuba diving |
| **Foreign travel/residence** | High-risk travel or residency |
| **Financial exposure** | Sum assured vs. income and assets; insurable interest |
| **Driving record** | MVR for high sums assured |

### Rate Classes (Table Rating)

Insurers classify lives into **rate classes**:

| Class | Description |
|---|---|
| **Preferred best / super preferred** | Very low risk; excellent health, lifestyle |
| **Preferred** | Low risk; good health |
| **Standard** | Average risk |
| **Substandard (table rated)** | Elevated risk; additional premium (e.g., Table A–D, or rated +25%, +50%, +100%) |
| **Declined** | Risk too high to underwrite |

### Medical Underwriting

- **Application questionnaire** — health, family history, lifestyle, occupation.
- **Attending Physician Statement (APS)** — for significant conditions.
- **Medical exams and lab tests** — blood, urine, for higher sums assured or certain ages.
- **Tele-underwriting / paramedical exams** — improve speed and accuracy.
- **Simplified issue** — no medical exam; higher premium.
- **Guaranteed issue** — no medical questions; for small amounts or group schemes; limited benefits in early years.

### Non-Medical (Financial) Underwriting

- Verify **insurable interest** (beneficiary relationship).
- Verify **sum assured vs. income/assets** (maximum multiple of income, e.g., 10–20×).
- Check **toxic/debt cover** appropriateness.
- For high sums assured: financial justification documents (income tax returns, net worth, business valuation).

## Rating and Premium

- **Premium = mortality cost + expenses + risk margin**, with investment return credited.
- **Level term** — premiums level over the term.
- **Annual renewable term** — premium increases by age.
- **Whole life** — level premium with cash value accumulation.

Key actuarial inputs:

- Mortality table (e.g., 2015 VBT, select and ultimate tables).
- Interest rate assumption.
- Expense load.
- Lapse assumptions.

## Policy Provisions

| Provision | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Grace period** | 30–31 days to pay premium after due date |
| **Reinstatement** | Relapse after lapse within limits (health evidence) |
| **Incontestability** | Insurer cannot contest after 2 years (except fraud in some jurisdictions) |
| **Suicide clause** | 2 years: death by suicide pays premiums returned only |
| **Beneficiary designation** | Revocable/irrevocable; per stirpes vs. per capita |
| **Cash value loan** | Borrow against cash value |
| **Non-forfeiture options** | Cash surrender, reduced paid-up, extended term |
| **Waiver of premium** | Premium waived on disability |
| **Accelerated benefits** | Early payment for terminal illness |
| **Riders** | Term additions, disability income, critical illness, accidental death |

## Life Insurance in the UK

- **Term assurance** (level, decreasing, increasing) is the most common protection product.
- **Whole of life** — often for inheritance tax planning, sometimes written in trust.
- **Endowments** — historic "with-profits" products.
- **Critical illness cover** — lump sum on diagnosis of listed conditions.
- **Income protection** — long-term disability income (replaces a portion of earnings).
- **FCA conduct rules** — advised sales through FCA-regulated advisers; consumer duty and fair value apply.
- **Protection distribution** — through advisers, banks, and increasingly direct/insurtech digital channels.

## Summary

- Life insurance provides **fixed sums on death** (and sometimes on survival or disability).
- Underwriting blends **medical, non-medical, and financial** risk assessment.
- Products range from **term to whole life**, and from guaranteed issue to fully underwritten.
- **Age, health, smoking, occupation, lifestyle, and sum assured** drive rating.
