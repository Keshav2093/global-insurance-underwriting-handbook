# 2.8 Personal Package & Risk Management

## Overview

Personal lines are increasingly sold and managed as **accounts** or **packages** rather than as standalone policies. A personal package typically combines:

- **Auto** (liability, physical damage, UM/UIM)
- **Home** (homeowners or dwelling fire)
- **Umbrella / excess liability**
- **Watercraft, RV, and other recreational vehicles**
- **Life, health, and accident products**
- **Identity theft and other personal risk products**

The economics of the package are favorable to the insurer:

- **Multi-policy discounts** increase retention.
- **Account bundling** reduces acquisition and servicing cost per policy.
- **Cross-selling** deepens customer relationships and lifetime value.
- **Data sharing** across the account improves underwriting and pricing.

## Why Package Underwriting Matters

| Benefit | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Retention** | Households with multiple products are far less likely to lapse |
| **Risk selection** | Viewing the whole household exposes risks hidden by single lines (e.g., a boat, a teenage driver, a rental property) |
| **Pricing synergies** | Multi-policy discounts are earned by account profitability |
| **Service efficiency** | One point of contact, one bill, one renewal |
| **Claims experience** | Claims across lines feed a household-level view |

The underwriter must therefore evaluate the **whole account**, not just the individual policy.

## Account Underwriting Approach

### Step 1 — Identify All Exposures

Collect the complete household picture:

- All vehicles, drivers, and auto use.
- All owned/rented dwellings (primary, second home, rental).
- Watercraft, RVs, ATVs, trailers.
- Pets; pools; trampolines.
- Business activities conducted at home.
- High-value personal property.
- Life, health, disability needs.

### Step 2 — Assess Household Risk Profile

| Dimension | Inputs |
|---|---|
| **Driving risk** | MVR for all drivers, claim history, telematics if available |
| **Property risk** | CLUE, condition, location hazards (flood, wind, wildfire), replacement cost |
| **Liability risk** | Pets, pools, watercraft, rental properties, umbrella needs |
| **Financial risk** | Credit (where legal), assets, continuity |
| **Behavioral risk** | Claims frequency, retention trend, complaint history |

### Step 3 — Package Pricing

- Apply **multi-policy and account discounts**.
- Identify **surcharges** for specific exposures (e.g., watercraft, second homes).
- Determine whether the account falls within **appetite** or needs referral.

### Step 4 — Retention and Growth Strategy

- At renewal, review the account for **coverage gaps** and **cross-sell** opportunities.
- Monitor **household tenure** and address complaints promptly.
- Use loss information to **re-price** rather than simply non-renew.

## Multi-Policy Discounts

Discounts vary by insurer and product but commonly include:

- **Auto + home** (or auto + renters) — the primary package discount.
- **Multi-vehicle** — additional vehicles.
- **Multi-line life/health** — bundling protection products.
- **Umbrella** — adding excess liability on top of the package.

Discounts are **regulated**:
- They must be filed and approved (US) as part of rate filings.
- They must not be unfairly discriminatory.
- They should be actuarially justified (loss cost savings, expense savings, retention value).

## Underwriting the Package: Red Flags

| Red Flag | Response |
|---|---|
| Multiple recent at-fault accidents across lines | Decline or surcharge; require loss-control actions |
| Significant claims frequency (e.g., 2+ claims in 3 years) | Tighten terms; increase deductibles; possibly non-renew |
| High-value unscheduled items | Require scheduling or limits reduction |
| Dog bite history | Exclude breed or decline; require umbrella |
| Unmanaged short-term rentals | Endorse or decline; require landlord product |
| Financial distress signals | Review payment performance; manage credit risk |
| Newly acquired high-risk exposures | Re-underwrite mid-term; quote or endorse |

## Consumer Risk Management

Beyond product bundling, personal lines serve **risk management** for the household:

- **Prevention** — loss-control advice (fire safety, water leak sensors, safe driving programs).
- **Transfer** — insurance and umbrella products.
- **Retention** — deductibles, self-insured options.
- **Financial planning** — life, disability, and long-term care to protect income and assets.

The modern personal lines insurer increasingly functions as a **household risk partner**, leveraging data (telematics, IoT, claims analytics) to reduce risk before it becomes a claim.

## Data and Technology in Personal Lines

| Technology | Use |
|---|---|
| **Telematics** | Driving behavior pricing and feedback |
| **Smart home sensors** | Leak/fire detection, discount qualification |
| **Credit/insurance scores** | Loss-propensity prediction (where lawful) |
| **Claim databases** | Household loss history (CLUE, A-Plus) |
| **Predictive models** | Conversion, retention, pricing, fraud |
| **Customer portals** | Self-service, document access, policy changes |

Underwriters must ensure models and data use comply with **fair discrimination** and **privacy** rules.

## Renewal Underwriting

Renewal is the most important retention event:

- **Evidence of change** — new drivers, new vehicles, home improvements, new claims.
- **Re-rate** — apply current rates to the account.
- **Check coverage adequacy** — inflation-adjusted replacement cost; updated liability needs.
- **Consider the account** — do not lose a good account over a single price point; balance profitability with retention.

## Summary

- Personal lines are best underwritten as **household accounts**, not single policies.
- **Package discounts and cross-sell** drive retention and profitability.
- Account underwriting identifies **hidden exposures** and manages the whole household risk profile.
- **Data and technology** enable more precise underwriting and pricing.
- Renewal is where the value of the account is realized — or lost.
