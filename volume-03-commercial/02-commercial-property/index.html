    3.2 Commercial Property¶

    Overview¶

    Commercial property insurance protects business-owned real and personal property against physical loss and resulting business interruption. It covers:

    • Buildings — the structure and permanent fixtures.
    • Business personal property — contents, stock, equipment, and improvements.
    • Business income (BI) / extra expense — lost revenue and continuing expenses after a covered loss.
    • Additional coverages — debris removal, ordinance/law, loss of rents, etc.

    The Commercial Property Program (ISO)¶

    The ISO commercial property program consists of:

    Component Purpose
    Building and Personal Property Coverage Form (BPP) Core direct damage coverage
    Business Income (and Extra Expense) Coverage Form Lost revenue and extra costs
    Cause of Loss Forms Basic, Broad, Special — define covered perils
    Conditions and Endorsements Coinsurance, valuation, deductibles, ordinance/law, etc.

    Building and Personal Property (BPP)¶

    Coverage What It Covers
    Building The structure, fixtures, permanently installed machinery (owned or insured under a lease), additions under construction, and building materials/equipment
    Business personal property Furniture, fixtures, machinery, equipment, stock, and other personal property owned or held in trust while on the described premises
    Property of others Personal property of others in the insured's care, custody, or control
    Additional coverages Debris removal, preservation of property, fire department charges, pollutant clean-up, etc.

    Business Income / Extra Expense¶

    • Business income — net income the business would have earned plus continuing normal operating expenses during the period of restoration.
    • Extra expense — necessary costs to continue operations (e.g., renting temporary space).
    • Period of restoration — from the loss until repaired/replaced/business reopened with reasonable speed.
    • Extended business income — protects against loss of income after reopening due to slow recovery of customers.
    • Loss of rents / rental value — for landlord exposures.

    Cause of Loss Forms¶

    Form Covered Perils
    Basic Fire, lightning, explosion, windstorm/hail, smoke, riot, vandalism, sprinkler leakage, etc. (named)
    Broad Basic plus falling objects, weight of snow/ice, water damage, etc. (named)
    Special (Open Perils) All risks of physical loss unless excluded — the most common for commercial

    Common exclusions: flood, earthquake, ordinance/law, wear and tear, rust/corrosion, mold, pollution (limited), nuclear, war, government action, and business interruption without direct physical damage (e.g., utility outage contingent BI requires endorsement).

    Valuation¶

    Basis Meaning
    Replacement Cost Cost to replace with like kind and quality, no depreciation
    Actual Cash Value Replacement cost less depreciation
    Functional Replacement Cost Cost of a modern equivalent that serves the same function
    Market Value / Agreed Value Stated or appraised value; used for special property

    Coinsurance¶

    The coinsurance clause requires the insured to carry insurance equal to a stated percentage (usually 80%, 90%, or 100%) of the property's value at the time of loss:

    Payment = Loss × (Insurance Carried ÷ Insurance Required)

    If the insured under-insures, the claim is proportionately reduced. Example:

    • Building value: \$1,000,000; 90% coinsurance requires \$900,000.
    • Insured carries \$600,000.
    • Loss: \$300,000.
    • Payment: \$300,000 × (\$600,000 ÷ \$900,000) = \$200,000.

    Underwriters must ensure values are adequate at inception and on change; agreed value endorsements waive coinsurance where the insurer has approved the stated values.

    COPE — The Core Underwriting Factors¶

    Factor Explanation
    Construction Fire-resistive, masonry, frame, wood, unprotected steel; building materials and finish
    Occupancy What the building is used for; hazards of operations (cooking, chemicals, storage)
    Protection Public fire protection (hydrants, fire department), private protection (sprinklers, alarms, extinguishers)
    Exposure Surrounding buildings and external hazards (wildfire, flood, wind, exposure from neighbors)

    Underwriting Commercial Property¶

    Key Underwriting Steps¶

    1. Verify values — SOV accuracy; replacement cost estimation; coinsurance compliance.
    2. Assess COPE — construction, occupancy, protection, exposure.
    3. Review loss history — frequency, severity, and causes of prior losses.
    4. Evaluate management — housekeeping, maintenance, safety culture, and risk engineering implementation.
    5. Identify catastrophes — flood zone, wildfire, wind/hail, earthquake; require appropriate deductibles or separate coverage.
    6. Check occupancy controls — hazardous operations, hot work, storage density, equipment maintenance.
    7. Set terms — limits, deductibles, coinsurance, valuation, special conditions (e.g., sprinkler warranty, protection requirements).

    Underwriting Red Flags¶

    • Underinsurance or a SOV that has not been updated.
    • Frame construction with no sprinklers.
    • Poor housekeeping; blocked exits; overloaded storage.
    • Deferred maintenance (roof, electrical, HVAC).
    • High fire load (combustibles) without protection.
    • Flood or wildfire zone exposure without required coverage/deductible.
    • Frequent small claims suggesting behavioral issues.

    Protection Requirements¶

    Conditions often warrant that fire protection systems be maintained in service:

    • Sprinkler warranty — require sprinkler system operational; breakage penalty.
    • Alarm requirements — fire/burglary alarms monitored.
    • Inspections and testing — mandated fire protection system testing and records.

    Business Income Underwriting¶

    • Review financial statements to verify exposure and determine appropriate period of restoration.
    • Assess dependence on a single location, supplier, or customer.
    • Contingent business income — covers losses from suppliers' or customers' premises (requires endorsement and specific exposure data).
    • Check utility dependence — power, water, data (endorsements for utility interruption).

    Endorsements and Special Forms¶

    Endorsement Purpose
    Ordinance or Law Demolition, increased cost of construction, loss of business income due to code compliance
    Flood / Earthquake Add coverage or provide separate policy
    Equipment Breakdown Mechanical/electrical breakdown (boiler and machinery)
    Spoilage Refrigerated stock loss
    Builder's Risk Property under construction
    Installation Floater Property in transit/at site
    Difference in Conditions (DIC) Broadens coverage beyond primary (often for flood/earthquake gap)
    Peak season / seasonal limits Adjust limits for seasonal inventory

    UK Commercial Property¶

    • Commercial combined insurance typically covers buildings, contents/stock, business interruption, and liability in one policy.
    • Sum insured for buildings is the rebuild cost; contents/stock at full value.
    • Average clause — similar to coinsurance: under-insurance reduces claims payment (e.g., 80% average).
    • Business interruption — indemnity period (e.g., 12, 24, 36 months), gross profit basis.
    • Property owners/landlord policies, shop/office policies, and commercial combined are common market products.
    • Flood is a standard covered peril in UK commercial property (no general flood exclusion), but high-flood-risk addresses are rated/located risk or may be declined.

    Summary¶

    • Commercial property covers buildings, contents, and business income.
    • COPE (construction, occupancy, protection, exposure) is the core analysis framework.
    • Coinsurance (average) penalizes underinsurance; value adequacy is critical.
    • Open perils (special) is the dominant cause of loss form.
    • Business interruption requires financial analysis and careful period-of-restoration setting.
