    3.3 Commercial General Liability¶

    Overview¶

    Commercial General Liability (CGL) protects businesses against third-party claims for bodily injury (BI) and property damage (PD), plus personal and advertising injury, arising from the insured's premises, operations, products, and completed work. CGL also covers the cost of defending such claims.

    CGL is the foundation of commercial liability coverage:

    • Premises liability — injury to visitors on the insured's premises.
    • Operations liability — injury or damage arising from ongoing operations.
    • Products liability — injury or damage caused by products sold or distributed.
    • Completed operations — injury or damage from work completed by the insured.
    • Personal and advertising injury — libel, slander, false arrest, copyright infringement in advertising.

    The CGL Policy Structure (ISO CG 00 01)¶

    The standard ISO CGL has two coverage forms:

    Form Coverage
    Occurrence (CG 00 01) Covers BI/PD caused by an occurrence (an accident) during the policy period, including for bodily injury that manifests after the period
    Claims-made (CG 00 02) Covers claims first made during the policy period (and reported within the extended reporting period)
    Claims-made (CG 00 33) Simplified claims-made variant

    Coverage A — Bodily Injury and Property Damage Liability¶

    • Who is insured: the named insured, employees (for acts within the scope of employment), partners/members/executives, and others as defined.
    • What is covered: sums the insured is legally obligated to pay as damages due to BI or PD caused by an occurrence.
    • Defense: the insurer has the right and duty to defend suits seeking such damages — defense costs are typically outside the limit for most CGL policies (though some are "defense within limits").

    Coverage B — Personal and Advertising Injury¶

    • Covers injury arising from offenses such as libel, slander, defamation, false arrest, wrongful eviction, invasion of privacy, and misappropriation of advertising ideas.
    • Subject to the insured's advertising activities; many exposures excluded for certain professions.

    Coverage C — Medical Payments¶

    • Pays medical expenses for bodily injury caused by an accident on the premises or arising from the insured's operations, regardless of fault.
    • Limited amounts (e.g., \$5,000 per person); no admission of liability.

    Key Definitions¶

    Term Meaning
    Occurrence An accident, including continuous or repeated exposure to substantially the same general harmful conditions
    Bodily injury Bodily injury, sickness, or disease sustained by a person, including death
    Property damage Physical injury to tangible property, or loss of use of tangible property not physically injured
    Personal injury Injury arising from specified offenses (libel, slander, false arrest, etc.)
    Advertising injury Injury from specified offenses in the course of advertising
    Products-completed operations BI/PD arising from products sold/distributed or work completed after possession is transferred; excluded from the general aggregate
    Insured The named insured and others as defined

    Coverage Triggers¶

    Trigger Meaning
    Occurrence Injury/damage must occur during the policy period
    Claims-made Claim must be first made during the policy period (and reported within the ERP)
    Retroactive date Claims-made policies only cover claims from events after the retroactive date

    Occurrence vs. Claims-Made — Underwriting Implications¶

    Factor Occurrence Claims-made
    Tail Insurer remains on risk for claims arising later from events during the policy period No tail unless the ERP is purchased (e.g., 1, 3, 5 years)
    Pricing Must anticipate long-tail development Pricing based on current claims made
    Risk Greater uncertainty for the insurer Greater certainty but tail risk transferred to the insured
    Best for Low-to-moderate tail lines (GL, auto) Long-tail and professional lines (malpractice, E&O, D&O, environmental)

    The choice between occurrence and claims-made is a significant underwriting and pricing decision.

    Aggregate Limits¶

    • General aggregate — the maximum the insurer pays for all BI/PD (except products-completed operations) during the policy period.
    • Products-completed operations aggregate — separate cap for products/completed ops claims.
    • Per-occurrence limit — the maximum for any one occurrence.
    • Personal and advertising injury limit — per person/organization.

    Example limits: \$1,000,000 per occurrence / \$2,000,000 general aggregate.

    Exclusions (Key)¶

    Exclusion Effect
    Expected or intended injury Not covered
    Contractual liability Only covered if assumed in an "insured contract"
    Liquor liability Excluded unless endorsements are added (host liquor, etc.)
    Workers compensation Excluded; covered by WC
    Pollution Broadly excluded; limited coverage for specific operations
    Aircraft, auto, watercraft Excluded; covered under other policies
    Mobile equipment Not covered as auto; covered as premises/operations
    Damage to your work / product Your own defective work/product not covered
    Damage to impaired property Your work/product that damages other property is limited
    Professional services Excluded; requires separate professional liability
    Product recall Excluded; requires recall coverage
    Punitive damages Not covered in many states

    Underwriting the CGL¶

    Key Underwriting Questions¶

    1. What are the operations? — class code, description of business.
    2. Where are the premises? — territory, condition, hazards on site.
    3. What products are sold or work completed? — products exposure; completed operations.
    4. Who are the customers/end users? — adjacent risk; contractual requirements.
    5. What is the claims experience? — loss runs (frequency and severity).
    6. What contractual obligations exist? — hold-harmless, additional insured requirements.
    7. What is the management quality? — safety culture, loss-control execution.

    Exposure Analysis¶

    Exposure Underwriting Information Needed
    Premises Square footage, occupancies, business operations on site, visitors/customers
    Operations Off-site work (contractors), supervision, subcontracting
    Products Product type, hazards, distribution, recalls history, quality control
    Completed operations Duration of exposure, warranties, statute of repose
    Personal/advertising Advertising practices, intellectual property exposure

    Underwriting Data Sources¶

    • Application (ACORD GL).
    • Loss runs (5 years preferred).
    • Financial statements.
    • Inspection/risk engineering report.
    • Contracts and certificates of insurance (for additional insured requirements).
    • Subcontractor agreements (for contractors).

    Rating the CGL¶

    • Premises/operations — rated on payroll, square footage, gross receipts, or number of units.
    • Products/completed operations — rated on gross receipts or sales.
    • Class codes — ISO/NCCI classification of the business operations.
    • Experience modification — relates to the insured's own loss experience.
    • Limits and deductibles — ILF (Increased Limits Factor) tables and SIR options.
    • Territory — litigation climate and hazard location.

    CGL Endorsements¶

    Endorsement Purpose
    Additional Insured Adds parties (owners, contractors, lessors) to coverage
    Primary and Noncontributory States the CGL is primary and does not contribute with the additional insured's policy
    Waiver of Subrogation Waives the insurer's right to recover from the additional insured
    Contractual Liability Broadens assumed contract coverage (limited/blanket)
    Liquor Liability Adds coverage for the sale of alcohol
    Pollution Adds limited or full pollution coverage for specific operations
    Employee Benefits Liability Covers errors in administering employee benefits
    Hired and Non-Owned Auto Provided when no commercial auto exists
    Cyber Liability Data breach response and liability (often separate program)
    Product Withdrawal/Recall Adds recall expense coverage

    Contractors' Liability (Special Focus)¶

    Contractors have a distinctive profile:

    • Operations exposure — worksites, supervision, tools.
    • Subcontracting — ensure downstream coverage and certificates.
    • Completed operations — long-tail exposure; statute of repose.
    • Contractual requirements — additional insureds, indemnity agreements.
    • X-C-U exclusions — known hazards (explosion, collapse, underground).
    • OCP (Owners and Contractors Protective) policies for owner-contractor relationships.

    UK Commercial Liability¶

    • Public and Products Liability (PL/Products) — similar to CGL; covers third-party BI/PD claims.
    • Employers' Liability (EL) — compulsory; covers employee injuries not covered by the state system.
    • Claims-made vs. occurrence — both used; PL often occurrence-based; professional and some liability lines claims-made.
    • Principal's Liability and Contractor's All Risks are used in construction.
    • FCA rules — fair value and consumer duty apply to smaller commercial customers; brokers must disclose.

    Summary¶

    • CGL covers BI, PD, personal/advertising injury, and medical payments for third parties.
    • Occurrence vs. claims-made is a fundamental coverage and underwriting choice.
    • Aggregates cap per-period exposure; products/completed operations are separately aggregated.
    • Underwriting analyzes premises, operations, products, completed ops, contracts, and claims experience.
    • Class codes, gross receipts, payroll, and experience drive rating.
