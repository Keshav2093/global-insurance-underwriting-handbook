    3.4 Commercial Auto¶

    Overview¶

    Commercial auto insurance covers vehicles owned, hired, or used in business. It protects against:

    • Liability — third-party BI/PD from auto accidents.
    • Physical damage — collision and comprehensive for owned vehicles.
    • Medical payments / PIP — occupant injuries.
    • Uninsured/underinsured motorist — where applicable.

    Commercial auto is needed by any business that owns vehicles, hires vehicles, or has employees driving personal vehicles on business.

    The Business Auto Policy (BAP)¶

    The standard ISO Business Auto Coverage Form (CA 00 01) includes:

    Section Coverage
    Coverage Symbols Defines which autos are covered (1–9, 19, etc.)
    Liability Coverage BI/PD, defense, covered autos
    Physical Damage Collision, comprehensive, specified causes
    Medical Payments Occupant medical expense
    Uninsured/Underinsured Motorists By state requirements
    Covered Auto Conditions Duties, valuation, other insurance

    Coverage Symbols¶

    The symbols determine which autos are covered:

    Symbol Autos Covered
    1 Any auto
    2 Owned autos only
    3 Owned private passenger autos only
    4 Owned autos other than private passenger (trucks)
    5 Owned autos subject to no-fault
    6 Owned autos subject to compulsory insurance
    7 Specifically described autos
    8 Hired autos only
    9 Non-owned autos only
    19 Mobile equipment subject to compulsory insurance

    Choosing the correct symbols is critical — symbol 7 covers only listed vehicles; symbol 1 (any auto) is the broadest but most expensive.

    Commercial Auto Exposures¶

    Exposure Description
    Owned autos Vehicles owned by the business, including private passenger, trucks, trailers
    Hired autos Vehicles leased, rented, or borrowed by the business
    Non-owned autos Vehicles owned by employees used in the business (e.g., salespeople driving personal cars)
    Garage operations For auto dealers, repair shops, and service stations (Garage Coverage Form)
    Motor carrier / trucking Fleet operations, owner-operators, cargo liability
    Public livery Taxis, limousines, for-hire passenger transport

    Underwriting Commercial Auto¶

    Key Underwriting Data¶

    Data Source
    Application Vehicle schedule, garaging location, use, radius
    MVRs All authorized drivers
    Loss runs Prior auto losses (frequency/severity)
    Financials Credit, business stability
    Fleet information Vehicle types, values, mileage, maintenance
    Driver files CDL (commercial driver's license) status, training

    Underwriting Considerations¶

    1. Fleet composition — vehicle types (autos, trucks, trailers, special equipment).
    2. Radius of operations — local vs. long-haul.
    3. Driver quality — CDL holders, training, accident/violation history.
    4. Use — business, delivery, transportation of goods, passengers.
    5. Garaging — secure parking, territory, crime rate.
    6. Maintenance — vehicle condition and maintenance records.
    7. Safety program — driver training, telematics, policies.
    8. Contractual requirements — additional insureds, certificates.

    Underwriting Red Flags¶

    • High driver turnover or unlicensed drivers.
    • MVR violations (speeding, DUI).
    • Frequent at-fault accidents.
    • Poor vehicle maintenance.
    • Long-haul routes through high-risk territories.
    • Uninsured operation or lapsed coverage.

    Trucking and Motor Carrier¶

    Motor carrier underwriting is specialized:

    Topic Underwriting Focus
    FMCSA authority Operating authority, safety ratings
    Driver qualifications CDL, medical certificates, drug/alcohol testing
    Hours of service Compliance with HOS rules
    Vehicle maintenance Inspection records, DOT inspections
    Cargo Type (general freight, hazardous, refrigerated), liability, cargo insurance
    Owner-operators Status (independent contractor vs. employee)
    Physical damage Truck/trailer values, deductibles
    Excess liability Umbrella/truckers excess for high limits

    The Truckers Coverage Form (ISO CA 00 20) is designed for motor carriers; the Motor Carrier Coverage Form (CA 00 20/21 variants) addresses owner-operators and leased vehicles.

    Garage Coverage¶

    The Garage Coverage Form (ISO CA 00 05) is used for:

    • Auto dealers, repair shops, service stations.
    • Covers garage operations liability (premises and operations), auto liability (symbols), and garagekeepers (customer vehicles).
    • Dealers physical damage covers inventory (new/used vehicles) while on the lot.

    Rating Commercial Auto¶

    Factor Basis
    Liability Vehicle type, radius, use, territory, driver class, fleet size
    Physical damage Vehicle value, make/model, territory, deductibles
    Cargo Cargo type, value, radius
    Experience Loss ratio / experience modification
    Limits Increased limits factors
    Deductible Choice of deductible
    Fleet discount Volume credit

    UK Commercial Motor¶

    • Fleet policies — multiple vehicles on one policy; rated by vehicle class, usage, drivers, claims history, territory.
    • Own goods — vehicles used to carry the insured's own goods (e.g., delivery vans).
    • Hired/rental — vehicles hired with driver or without.
    • Trade (garage) — motor traders insurance for vehicles in the dealer's custody.
    • FCA rules — fair value, pricing rules, and conduct for commercial customers.

    Claims Considerations¶

    • Coverage — symbol check, driver authorization, use.
    • Liability investigation — fault determination, police reports.
    • Physical damage — appraisal, total loss, salvage.
    • Cargo claims — loss/damage in transit, carrier liability.
    • Subrogation — recover from at-fault third parties.
    • Fraud — staged accidents, inflated claims, fake cargo losses.

    Summary¶

    • Commercial auto covers owned, hired, and non-owned vehicles for liability and physical damage.
    • Symbols define coverage scope and are a frequent source of errors.
    • Trucking, garage, and public livery are specialized exposures with separate forms.
    • Underwriting focuses on fleet, drivers, use, radius, maintenance, and claims history.
    • MVRs, loss runs, and safety programs are the core data sources.
