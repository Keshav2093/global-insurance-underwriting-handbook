    2.3 Homeowners Insurance¶

    Overview¶

    Homeowners insurance combines property and liability coverages for owner-occupied dwellings. It protects:

    • The dwelling, other structures, personal property, and loss of use.
    • The owner's personal liability for bodily injury or property damage to others.
    • Medical payments for minor injuries to others.

    Homeowners is typically required by mortgage lenders for financed homes. It is one of the most rate-regulated personal lines products.

    The Homeowners (HO) Forms¶

    The standard US ISO homeowners forms are:

    Form Dwelling Occupancy Property Perils
    HO-2 (Broad) Owner-occupied 1–4 family Named perils (16)
    HO-3 (Special) Owner-occupied 1–4 family Open perils for dwelling; named perils for personal property
    HO-5 (Comprehensive) Owner-occupied Open perils for dwelling and personal property
    HO-6 (Condo) Condominium unit owner Personal property (open or named perils) + unit owner additions
    HO-7 (Mobile Home) Mobile/manufactured home Perils as HO-3
    HO-8 (Modified/Older Home) Owner-occupied older home Named perils; repair cost basis

    Many insurers use their own "homeowners package" forms modeled on these, or choose "modified" forms (HO-3 with HO-5-style coverage for contents).

    Coverages Under HO-3 (Illustrative)¶

    Section I — Property¶

    Coverage Protection
    A — Dwelling Structure and attached structures (garage, deck, built-in appliances)
    B — Other Structures Detached structures (shed, fence, detached garage) — typically 10% of Coverage A
    C — Personal Property Contents and personal belongings — typically 50–70% of Coverage A
    D — Loss of Use Additional living expenses and fair rental value when the dwelling is uninhabitable — typically 20–30% of Coverage A

    Named perils for contents in HO-3 include: fire, lightning, windstorm, hail, explosion, riot/civil commotion, aircraft, vehicles, smoke, vandalism, theft, and others.

    Open perils for the dwelling cover all risks of physical loss unless specifically excluded.

    Common Exclusions¶

    • Flood (surface water, overflow, tidal water, etc.) — separate flood policy (NFIP/private).
    • Earthquake — separate earthquake endorsement/policy.
    • Wear and tear, deterioration, latent defect — excluded as "not a risk of physical loss" or expressly excluded.
    • Mold — limited or excluded beyond specified amounts.
    • Ordinance or law — cost to rebuild to current code (limited; increase cover).
    • Intentional loss, neglect, and power failure.
    • Business property and business use (limited).
    • Animal liability — dog bites covered but certain breeds may be excluded.

    Valuation¶

    • Dwelling — replacement cost (up to the limit) with extended replacement cost endorsements (e.g., 125% or 150%).
    • Personal property — replacement cost or actual cash value (ACV) option.
    • HO-8 — repair cost basis.
    • Inflation guard — automatic annual adjustment.

    Coinsurance / Adequate Insurance¶

    Most homeowners forms do not apply a coinsurance penalty as such, but payment is limited to the limit plus the extended replacement cost percentage. Underinsuring leaves the insured personally exposed. Underwriters estimate the replacement cost using a replacement-cost estimator (square footage, construction quality, features, local building costs).

    Section II — Liability¶

    Coverage Protection
    E — Personal Liability Bodily injury or property damage to others caused by an occurrence on or off the insured premises; includes defense costs
    F — Medical Payments to Others Minor injuries to guests, typically \$1,000–\$5,000 per person

    Exclusions to Liability¶

    • Intentional acts.
    • Business activities (some may be covered by endorsement or separate policy).
    • Motor vehicles (covered by auto policy).
    • Watercraft above stated size/power (may be added by endorsement).
    • Aircraft.
    • Workers compensation / employment liability.
    • Professional services.

    Endorsements¶

    Common endorsements include:

    • Scheduled personal property (jewelry, fine arts, firearms, collectibles).
    • Water backup and sump overflow.
    • Service line coverage.
    • Equipment breakdown.
    • Identity theft.
    • Home business extensions.
    • Increased replacement cost and ordinance/law coverage.
    • Dog liability (underwritten by breed).
    • Additional insureds (e.g., landlord, mortgagee).

    Rating Homeowners¶

    The premium is computed from:

    Factor Explanation
    Replacement cost (Coverage A) The dominant rating factor; estimated by RCE tools
    Territory (protection class/ISO) Fire protection district, claim frequency, litigation
    Construction Frame vs. masonry vs. manufactured
    Age and condition Roof age, HVAC/electrical/plumbing updates
    Claims history CLUE and internal loss history
    Credit-based score In many states (legally restricted in others)
    Deductible % based deductibles (e.g., 1%, 2%, 5% of Coverage A) common in catastrophe states
    Discounts Multi-policy, alarm, sprinkler, impact-resistant roof, no-claims, new home, retirement community
    Catastrophe surcharges Wind/hail, hurricane, wildfire risk areas

    Underwriting Homeowners¶

    Key Underwriting Checks¶

    1. Replacement cost adequacy — ensure Coverage A is sufficient; recommend extended replacement cost.
    2. Condition of the home — age, roof, electrical, plumbing, heating, and apparent maintenance.
    3. Claims history — prior losses (CLUE) for frequency and severity.
    4. Dogs and exotic pets — breed and bite history.
    5. Trampolines, pools, and playgrounds — attractive nuisance and liability exposure.
    6. Business use — home offices, inventory, daycare, rental of rooms/units.
    7. Location hazards — flood zone, wildfire zone, earthquake zone, wind/hail territory; require appropriate coverage or endorsements.
    8. Vacancy — a vacant home is a higher risk; long vacancy may require special coverage or be declined.
    9. Short-term rentals — Airbnb/VRBO require special endorsement or separate policy.
    10. High-value homes — require appraisal/inspection-based replacement cost, and may fall outside standard guidelines.

    Inspection Triggers¶

    Inspections (virtual or physical) are typically required when:

    • Coverage A exceeds a threshold (e.g., \$750k–\$1M).
    • Older construction (e.g., 40+ years) without updates.
    • High-value or specialty risks.
    • The application raises questions (unknown square footage, recent purchase, etc.).
    • Claims history suggests condition issues.

    Underwriting Declines¶

    • Commercial or excessive business exposure.
    • Uninsurable condition (severe deferred maintenance, active leaks, unsafe structures).
    • Certain dog breeds / bite history (by insurer rules).
    • Illegal activity or occupation hazards.
    • Failure to maintain required minimum deductibles in catastrophe areas.
    • Non-payment or fraud history.

    Flood and Earthquake¶

    Flood¶

    • NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program) — federal program; separate policy; 30-day waiting period; limit of \$250k building / \$100k contents (single-family).
    • Private flood insurance — growing market; higher limits and broader terms.
    • Determining flood risk: FEMA flood zone maps (A, V, X, etc.), elevation, history.
    • Underwriters require flood coverage for homes in Special Flood Hazard Areas (A/V zones) when financing.

    Earthquake¶

    • Typically an endorsement or separate policy with a percentage deductible (e.g., 10–15% of Coverage A).
    • Offered by insurers or state pools (e.g., California Earthquake Authority, CEA).
    • Not required by lenders but recommended in seismic zones.

    UK Home Insurance¶

    UK home insurance has two main components, typically sold as one policy:

    Component Covers
    Buildings Structure, permanent fixtures, and fittings; sum insured based on rebuild cost (not market value)
    Contents Personal belongings; sum insured or "unlimited" with limits on valuables

    Key UK special features:

    • Sum insured — rebuild cost for buildings; contents value for contents. Flood risk is included in standard buildings cover.
    • Excess — compulsory and voluntary excesses.
    • New-for-old — most contents policies pay replacement value.
    • Accidental damage — optional extension to buildings and contents.
    • Home emergency and legal expenses are common add-ons.
    • FCA pricing rules — renewal pricing cannot be higher than new business (price walking ban).
    • Flood Re — the UK government-backed scheme that makes flood cover available through participating insurers for eligible homes.

    Claims Considerations¶

    • Property claims — fire, water damage, theft, wind/hail; adjusters verify coverage, cause, and quantum (ACV vs. RCV; depreciation recovery upon repair).
    • Liability claims — third-party injury on premises, dog bites; liability investigation and defense.
    • Catastrophe claims — hurricanes, tornadoes, wildfires, floods mobilize surge adjusters and special handling.
    • Fraud — content inflation, arson-for-profit, staged "slip and fall", contractor fraud.

    Summary¶

    • Homeowners binds property and liability cover in one product.
    • Forms differ by occupancy and peril basis (HO-2, HO-3, HO-5, HO-6, HO-7, HO-8).
    • Replacement cost adequacy is the central underwriting concern.
    • Catastrophe exposure (flood, wind, earthquake, wildfire) requires special handling and endorsements.
    • UK home insurance operates with different terminology and flood scheme (Flood Re).
