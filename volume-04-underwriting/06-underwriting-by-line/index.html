    4.6 Underwriting by Line¶

    Overview¶

    Every insurance line has its own risk profile, loss drivers, and underwriting techniques. This chapter provides a cross-line reference of the key underwriting considerations that were developed in Volumes 2 and 3.

    Property — Personal and Commercial¶

    Factor Personal Commercial
    Core framework COPE COPE
    Primary perils Fire, theft, wind, water Fire, water, business interruption
    Valuation Replacement cost (owner-occupied); ACV for rentals Replacement cost; coinsurance; agreed value
    Main data Application, CLUE, inspection Application, SOV, loss runs, engineering
    Key loss control Mitigation devices, construction Sprinklers, alarms, maintenance programs

    Auto — Personal and Commercial¶

    Factor Personal Commercial
    Rating basis Driver + vehicle + territory Symbol + fleet + radius + use
    Main data MVR, CLUE, telematics MVRs, loss runs, fleet schedule, CDL files
    Key exposures Teen drivers, high-value vehicles Long-haul, cargo, hired/non-owned
    Loss control Driver education, telematics Driver training, telematics, maintenance

    General Liability¶

    Exposure Underwriting Emphasis
    Premises Condition, visitors, slip/trip, security
    Operations Contractors, supervision, subcontracting
    Products Product type, distribution, recall history, QC
    Completed operations Long-tail; statute of repose
    Contracts Indemnity, additional insureds
    Litigation climate Territory, legal environment

    Workers Compensation¶

    • Payroll and class codes drive premium.
    • Experience modification reflects loss history.
    • Underwriting emphasizes safety culture, return-to-work, drug-free workplace, and claims management.
    • State benefit levels and litigation environment matter.

    Specialty Lines¶

    Line Key Underwriting Factors
    Ocean marine Vessel, route, cargo, P&I
    Inland marine Transit, bailees, installation, EDP
    Aviation Pilot, aircraft, use, maintenance
    Surety Financial strength, capacity, experience
    Professional liability Qualifications, claims history, risk controls
    D&O Financials, governance, securities exposure
    Cyber Security controls, data sensitivity, prior losses
    Environmental Site assessments, operations, remediation

    Life and Health¶

    Product Underwriting Emphasis
    Individual life Age, health, lifestyle, insurable interest, amount
    Disability Occupation, income, health, financial motivation
    Critical illness Health history, family history, age
    Long-term care Age, health, cognitive/functional status
    Group benefits Participation, group demographics, employer stability

    The Underwriting "Toolbox" by Line¶

    Tool Lines Where Most Used
    COPE analysis Property (personal and commercial)
    MVR Auto (personal and commercial)
    CLUE / loss databases Property, auto, package
    Loss runs All commercial
    Financial statements Commercial, surety, D&O, key person
    Credit-based scores Personal lines where lawful
    Medical underwriting Life, health, disability
    Engineering surveys Commercial property, energy, marine
    Technical questionnaires Cyber, professional, environmental
    Inspection requirements Property, high-value personal

    Line-Specific Red Flags¶

    Line Red Flags
    Property Underinsurance, no sprinklers in high hazard, poor housekeeping, deferred maintenance
    Auto DUI, violations, unlicensed drivers, long-haul exposure, poor maintenance
    GL Frequent small claims, products exposure without QC, litigation history
    WC High frequency, misclassification, no return-to-work, fraud indicators
    Specialty Inadequate controls (cyber), prior securities claims (D&O), weak financials (surety)
    Life/health Hazardous avocations, substance abuse, oversized requests, financial motivation

    Integrated (Package) Underwriting¶

    Packages must be underwritten across lines:

    • Verify coverage consistency (limits, premises, operations).
    • Look for cross-line signals (e.g., frequent auto claims in a commercial account with labor issues).
    • Use package credits only where booking economics justify them.
    • Ensure no coverage gaps between property, liability, auto, and specialty needs.

    Summary¶

    • Each line has distinct loss drivers and techniques.
    • Data sources and tools map to lines (MVR → auto, CLUE → property, loss runs → all).
    • Red flags are line-specific but share a behavioral theme.
    • Packages require integrated underwriting.
    • The underwriter develops line expertise on top of core principles.
