    1.6 Insurance Operations¶

    The Policy Lifecycle¶

    Insurance operations revolve around the policy lifecycle — the sequence of activities from marketing through claims:

    1. Marketing & distribution — identifying and reaching buyers.
    2. Submission & application — gathering information and requesting coverage.
    3. Underwriting — evaluation, selection, and pricing.
    4. Rating & issuance — computing premium and issuing the policy.
    5. Policy administration — endorsements, renewals, cancellations, billing.
    6. Claims — FNOL investigation, evaluation, payment, subrogation.
    7. Analytics & feedback — loss data, experience, and pricing updates.

    Functional Overview¶

    Function Core Activities
    Distribution Agents, brokers, direct channels; binding; submissions
    Underwriting Appetite, risk evaluation, selection, pricing, terms, documentation
    Actuarial/Pricing Rate making, reserve estimation, model development
    Policy Admin Issuance, endorsements, renewals, billing, premium audit
    Claims FNOL, investigation, coverage analysis, evaluation, settlement, subrogation, SIU
    Risk Engineering/Loss Control Inspections, engineering analysis, recommendations
    Reinsurance Program design, placement, recoveries
    Finance/Accounting Premium accounting, reserves, reporting
    IT/Operations Systems, data, automation, servicing
    Compliance Regulatory, market conduct, privacy, complaints
    HR/Learning Staffing, training, competency

    Distribution¶

    Distribution channels connect buyers and insurers:

    • Independent agents represent multiple insurers and own the book.
    • Captive agents represent one insurer.
    • Brokers represent the insured and place business with insurers.
    • Direct — telephone, online, affinity groups.
    • Bancassurance — banks distribute insurance products.
    • MGAs / coverholders — delegated authority to underwrite, bind, and settle.

    The distribution channel affects:

    • Acquisition cost — commissions and volume.
    • Data quality — submission completeness.
    • Loss experience — channel loyalty and risk tolerance.

    Underwriting¶

    Underwriting is the selection and pricing of risk. It involves:

    1. Appetite setting — what the insurer will and will not write.
    2. Submission intake — collecting applications, inspections, financials, loss runs.
    3. Risk evaluation — analyzing hazards and exposures.
    4. Selection — accept, decline, or modify.
    5. Pricing and terms — applying rates and setting conditions.
    6. Documentation — the underwriting file.
    7. Portfolio management — monitoring the book.

    Underwriting is covered comprehensively in Volume 4.

    Rating & Pricing¶

    Rating translates the underwriting decision into a premium:

    • Manual rating — published rates by class.
    • Individual risk rating — experience and schedule adjustments.
    • Actuarial pricing — prospective loss costs and rating factors.

    Pricing is covered in Volume 5.

    Policy Administration¶

    Policy administration manages the policy after binding:

    • Issuance — producing the policy document accurately.
    • Endorsements — changes to coverage, limits, or insureds.
    • Renewals — continuation, re-rating, and non-renewal notices.
    • Cancellations — mid-term, pro-rata vs. short-rate.
    • Premium transactions — billing, payments, refunds, audits.

    Policy administration is covered in Volume 6.

    Claims¶

    Claims operations:

    • FNOL — first notice of loss capture.
    • Coverage analysis — determine if the loss is covered.
    • Investigation — facts, causation, liability, damages.
    • Evaluation — reserving and valuation of the claim.
    • Settlement/denial — payment, repair, or denial with documentation.
    • Subrogation and recoveries — pursuing third parties.
    • SIU — fraud detection and investigation.

    Claims is covered in Volume 7.

    Risk Engineering and Loss Control¶

    • Inspections — physical or virtual surveys to identify hazards.
    • Recommendations — requiring or recommending improvements (wiring, alarms, sprinklers, safety programs).
    • Engineering services — for complex industrial and construction risks.
    • Post-loss services — business continuity, mitigation.

    Risk engineering both reduces losses and informs underwriting decisions.

    Reinsurance¶

    Reinsurance supports the insurer's capacity:

    • Design of the reinsurance program (treaty, facultative, proportional, XOL).
    • Placement — securing markets and terms.
    • Recoveries — collecting from reinsurers after losses.
    • Cedant management — monitoring the net account.

    See 1.4 The Insurance Market for reinsurance structures.

    Data, Systems, and Automation¶

    Modern insurance operations run on:

    • Policy administration systems — the system of record.
    • Rating engines — automated rating and rules.
    • Claims systems — FNOL, reserving, settlement.
    • Data warehouses and analytics — experience, portfolio management, pricing.
    • Document management — versions, filings, compliance.
    • APIs and portals — agent portals, customer self-service.

    Automation trends include straight-through processing (STP), telematics, computer vision inspection, and machine-learning pricing. Automation improves efficiency but requires governance and control for fairness and accuracy.

    The Operating Model¶

    Element Description
    Process End-to-end workflows with clear hand-offs
    People Underwriters, technicians, adjusters, actuaries, support
    Technology Systems and data that enable the process
    Governance Delegated authority, controls, audits, compliance

    Delegated Authority¶

    Underwriting authority is delegated by limits:

    • Binders/authorities — agents and MGAs may bind within specified limits.
    • Underwriter authority — individual limits based on competency and experience.
    • Referral processes — escalation above authority or outside guidelines.
    • Audit — regular review of delegated decisions.

    Controls¶

    • Four-eyes — review of large or unusual risks.
    • Peer review — checking consistency.
    • Post-binding audit — retrospective file review.
    • Regulatory compliance — rate/rule/form usage verification.

    Operational Metrics¶

    Metric Purpose
    New business / renewal counts Volume and flow
    Quotes to bind ratio Conversion
    Turnaround time (TAT) Service speed
    Inspection completion Data quality
    Premium audit results Premium accuracy
    Endorsement accuracy Administrative quality
    Complaints Customer experience
    Lost policy ratio Retention
    Expense ratio Efficiency
    Loss ratio and combined ratio Profitability

    Key Integration Points¶

    • Underwriting ↔ Claims: loss feedback shapes pricing and selection.
    • Underwriting ↔ Actuarial: rates and models depend on underwriting data quality.
    • Underwriting ↔ Policy Admin: accurate issuance avoids E&O and regulatory issues.
    • Underwriting ↔ Reinsurance: net retention and capacity depend on the program.
    • Underwriting ↔ Risk Engineering: inspections support risk selection.

    Summary¶

    • Insurance operations are organized around the policy lifecycle.
    • Underwriting is the core risk-selection and pricing function.
    • Actuarial, policy admin, claims, risk engineering, and reinsurance are the supporting pillars.
    • Data and systems increasingly enable speed and accuracy.
    • Governance and controls protect both the insurer and the insured.
