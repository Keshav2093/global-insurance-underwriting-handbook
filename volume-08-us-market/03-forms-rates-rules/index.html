    8.3 Forms, Rates & Rules¶

    Overview¶

    States regulate the forms (policy wordings), rates (pricing), and rules (classification and eligibility standards) used by insurers. The filing system and approval standards differ by state and line of business — personal lines are more strictly controlled; commercial and surplus lines allow greater freedom.

    The Three Components¶

    Component Definition
    Forms Policy language — insuring agreements, exclusions, conditions, endorsements
    Rates Premium pricing — base rates, rating factors, schedules
    Rules Eligibility, classification, coverage rules, underwriting restrictions

    Filing Systems¶

    System How It Works Use
    Prior approval Rates/forms effective only after regulator approval Personal lines in many states
    File-and-use Filed; use after a waiting period Common hybrid
    Use-and-file Used; filed after effective date Commercial lines
    Open competition No filing; compliance with standards Some commercial states
    Flex rating Pre-approved band; changes outside need filing A few states

    Standards for Approval¶

    Rates must not be:

    • Excessive — above what is justified by expected losses and costs.
    • Inadequate — below the cost of coverage and expenses.
    • Unfairly discriminatory — differences not related to risk.

    Forms are reviewed for:

    • Compliance with state insurance law.
    • Clarity — no misleading or deceptive language.
    • Consistency with filed rates and rules.
    • Public policy — no provisions contrary to law.

    The Filing Process¶

    1. Prepare filing (forms, rates, rules, actuarial support)
2. File with the state (SERFF or paper)
3. Regulator review (approval, modification, rejection)
4. Implement on approval or after waiting period
5. Maintain and update (revisions, withdrawals)

    SERFF (System for Electronic Rate and Form Filing) is the NAIC's e-filing platform.

    What Must Be Filed¶

    Filing Typical Requirement
    New forms Policy and endorsement forms
    Rate changes Base rates, rating factors
    Rule changes Eligibility, classifications
    New products Full package filing
    Program changes Deviations from bureau filings

    Large commercial insureds and surplus lines may be exempt from form/rate filings.

    Advisory Organizations¶

    Organization Role
    ISO (Insurance Services Office) Commercial and personal forms, advisory rates
    NCCI Workers compensation forms and class rates
    AAIS Database and forms services
    State rating bureaus Some states retain approved bureaus

    Insurers may adopt bureau filings or file independently (deviations).

    Rate Filings — Actuarial Support¶

    Element Support
    Experience data Losses, exposure, premium history
    Trend Loss cost trend and inflation
    Development Loss development to ultimate
    Credibility Volume-weighted blending
    Expenses Acquisition, overhead, premium taxes
    Profit/risk load Target return and volatility
    Loss reserve margin Provision for adverse deviation

    Implementation Controls¶

    Control Purpose
    Approval tracking Effective dates per state
    Version control Correct form edition in force
    Rate tables Approved rates loaded correctly
    System updates Rating engine aligned with filings
    Audit trail Filing status and approvals documented

    Common Filing Issues¶

    Issue Impact
    Late filing Use of unapproved rates/forms
    Incorrect rate table Over/undercharging
    Form vs. rule mismatch Underwriting errors
    Missing state-specific endorsement Non-compliance
    No actuarial support Rejection or re-filing

    Summary¶

    • Forms, rates, and rules are filed and regulated per state.
    • Approval standards — not excessive, inadequate, or unfairly discriminatory.
    • SERFF and bureau filings streamline the process.
    • Actuarial support justifies rate changes.
    • Controls align approved filings with actual issuance.
