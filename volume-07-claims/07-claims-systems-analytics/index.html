    7.7 Claims Systems & Analytics¶

    Overview¶

    Claims operations depend on systems that handle notification, workflow, payments, and documents, and on analytics that detect fraud, predict outcomes, and measure performance. Good systems and data make claims faster, fairer, and more accurate.

    The Claims Management System (CMS)¶

    Function Description
    FNOL intake Capture, triage, and route claims
    Claim file Parties, coverage, documents, notes
    Reserving Case reserve setup and updates
    Workflow Assignments, approvals, task management
    Payments Checks, EFT, authority, releases
    Litigation management Counsel, budgets, deadlines
    Recovery tracking Subrogation, salvage, reinsurance
    Reporting Regulatory, management, performance

    Claims Data¶

    Data Type Use
    Policy data Coverage verification, limits
    Claim data Facts, parties, damages
    Payment data Reserves, payouts, expenses
    Litigation data Counsel, fees, outcomes
    Fraud data Red flags, SIU cases
    External data Credit, weather, police, medical

    Data quality controls — validation, deduplication, reconciliation — apply as in Volume 6.

    Analytics in Claims¶

    Application Description
    Fraud detection Rules and models flag suspicious claims
    Triaging Route claims by complexity and severity
    Reserve estimation Predictive models for reserve ranges
    Settlement prediction Likely outcome and value ranges
    Litigation prediction Verdict and cost forecasts
    Customer scoring Complexity, service needs
    Hotspot mapping Catastrophe and weather response

    Fraud Detection¶

    Indicator Example
    Late reporting Delay with suspicious timing
    Inconsistent story Changing details between statements
    Staged/planned events Common patterns (auto, slip-and-fall)
    Exaggerated damage/demands Built-up losses
    False documentation Forged bills, medical reports
    Relationships Claimant-insured connections
    MVR/prior claims History of similar losses

    Refer suspicious claims to Special Investigation Unit (SIU).

    The SIU Process¶

    Referral → Review → Investigation → Conclusion → Action
    Step Activity
    Referral Adjuster flags indicators
    Review SIU confirms or clears
    Investigation Surveillance, statements, records
    Conclusion Deny, prosecute, or close
    Action Referral to law enforcement if criminal

    Automation¶

    Automation Benefits
    FNOL auto-adjudication Simple claims settled instantly
    Document OCR Faster intake and indexing
    Workflow routing Claim to the right adjuster
    Payment automation Fewer errors, faster payments
    Chatbots First-line customer service
    Robotic process automation (RPA) Repetitive tasks (data entry, checks)

    Claims Analytics Metrics¶

    Metric Use
    Fraud referral rate SIU volume and culling
    Reserve accuracy Settlements vs. reserves
    Cycle time Speed of resolution
    Cost per claim Indemnity + expenses
    Recovery rate Subrogation/salvage success
    Customer satisfaction NPS, complaints
    Litigation rate Claims ending in suit

    Systems Integration¶

    Interface Purpose
    Policy system Coverage, limits, status
    Billing Premium and deductible details
    Finance Payments, GL posting
    Reinsurance Ceded claim reporting
    Fraud databases Industry loss and fraud sharing
    External vendors Repair networks, medical, experts

    Common System Issues¶

    Issue Impact
    Stale policy data Wrong coverage decisions
    Manual data entry Errors and delays
    Siloed systems No single claim view
    Poor document capture Evidence conflicts
    Uncontrolled access Privacy and security risk

    Summary¶

    • The CMS manages notification through closure.
    • Analytics detect fraud, predict outcomes, and guide triage.
    • SIU investigates suspicious claims.
    • Automation improves speed, accuracy, and cost.
    • Integration and data quality are foundational.
