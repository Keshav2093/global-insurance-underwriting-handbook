    7.4 Claims Resolution¶

    Overview¶

    Resolution is the step where the claim is settled, litigated, or denied. The goal is a fair, prompt, and accurate outcome — paying what is owed, defending when appropriate, and denying only with sound basis.

    Resolution Options¶

    Outcome Description
    Settlement Agreement on damages; payment and release
    Negotiated compromise Resolution without full trial value
    Denial Claim not covered or not valid
    Litigation Underlying claim goes to court
    Alternative dispute resolution Mediation, arbitration, mini-trial
    Structured settlement Payments over time (liability, workers comp)

    Settlement Authority¶

    Level Authority
    Adjuster Settle within delegated limit
    Supervisor/manager Higher limits; approvals
    Claims committee Large or complex claims
    Home office Very large, class-exposure, or unusual

    Authority is delegated and documented. Settling outside authority is a serious control breach.

    The Settlement Decision¶

    Factors in valuing and settling:

    Factor Consideration
    Liability Probability of adverse verdict or finding
    Damages Past/future medical, wage loss, pain and suffering
    Coverage Limits, exclusions, defense costs
    Comparative fault Allocation of responsibility
    Jury exposure Venue, sympathy, inflation of awards
    Costs of defense Litigation expense to verdict
    Collectibility Ability to pay judgment
    Claimant Representation, demands, credibility

    Negotiation¶

    Practice Description
    Anchor with evidence Open with reasoned, documented value
    Know authority Clear internal limit before negotiating
    Document offers All demands and offers recorded
    Time discipline Deadlines, litigation pressure
    Fair dealing No bad faith, no unreasonable offers

    Payment and Release¶

    The settlement is completed with:

    Document Purpose
    Release Claimant releases claims against insured/insurer
    Settlement agreement Terms, amounts, confidentiality
    Indemnity clause Protection for related future claims
    Dismissal (if in suit) Court dismissal with prejudice
    Check / EFT Payment issued to proper parties

    Denials¶

    A denial must be:

    Requirement Practice
    Timely Within regulatory timeframes
    In writing Clear statement of decision
    Reasoned Explain coverage basis and policy terms
    Fair Based on complete investigation
    Documented File supports the decision

    Denial is a last resort after full investigation — premature or unfounded denials create bad faith exposure.

    Litigation and Defense¶

    Element Description
    Duty to defend Broader than duty to indemnify
    Panel counsel Approved defense attorneys
    Litigation management Budgets, strategies, settlement authority
    ROR/declaratory Coverage resolution parallel to defense
    Appeals Post-verdict appellate risk management

    ADR¶

    Method Use
    Mediation Facilitated negotiation; non-binding
    Arbitration Binding decision by arbitrator
    Summary jury trial Advisory verdict to guide settlement
    Early neutral evaluation Expert opinion on value

    ADR is often faster, cheaper, and more controllable than trial.

    Large and Complex Claims¶

    Feature Requirement
    Catastrophe Surge teams, fast triage, well-defined process
    Class actions Coverage strategy, defense coordination
    Environmental Multi-party, long-tail exposures, coverage issues
    Cyber Incident response, notification obligations, specialists

    Documentation of Resolution¶

    The file must record:

    • The basis of the decision (evidence, liability, damages).
    • Authority and approvals.
    • All offers, demands, and counter-offers.
    • Settlement terms or denial reasons.
    • Payments and releases.
    • Communications with the insured and claimant.

    Summary¶

    • Resolution is settlement, negotiation, ADR, litigation, or denial.
    • Authority limits must be respected and documented.
    • Settlements end claims with release and payment.
    • Denials require full investigation and clear explanation.
    • Documentation protects every decision.
