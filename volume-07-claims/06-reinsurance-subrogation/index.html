    7.6 Reinsurance & Subrogation¶

    Overview¶

    Recoveries reduce the net cost of a claim. Reinsurance shifts loss to the reinsurer; subrogation recovers from responsible third parties; salvage recovers from damaged property. Effective recovery management materially improves loss ratios.

    The Recovery Concept¶

    Gross loss
  − Reinsurance recovery (ceded share)
  − Subrogation recovery (third party)
  − Salvage recovery (property)
  = Net cost to the insurer

    Reinsurance in Claims¶

    Concept Description
    Ceded loss The reinsurer's share per the treaty/agreement
    Retention The insurer's own net retention
    Cession to quota share Fixed percentage of every loss
    Excess of loss Reinsurer pays above the retention per occurrence
    Per-risk vs. per-occurrence Attachment basis
    Recoverables Amount due from reinsurer
    Bordereaux Periodic claim reporting to reinsurers

    Claim Reporting to Reinsurers¶

    Requirement Practice
    Notification Advise reinsurers of claims likely to hit the treaty
    Documentation Claim files, reserves, settlement rationale
    Bordereaux Periodic listing of ceded claims
    Settlement Bill recoverables after payment
    Approval rights Follow the treaty — settlement authority may require reinsurer consent

    Treaty and facultative certificates define the claims cooperation and follow-the-fortunes clauses.

    Subrogation¶

    Subrogation gives the insurer the right to step into the insured's position and recover what it paid from the party responsible for the loss.

    Element Description
    Basis Equitable and contractual doctrine
    Insured must not prejudice No release of wrongdoer without consent
    Deductible The insured's deductible may be recoverable first
    Waiver of subrogation Contractual waiver; common in construction
    Limitations Preserves rights; prevent waiver

    Subrogation Process¶

    1. Identify potential recovery (third-party fault, product, negligence)
2. Preserve evidence and legal deadlines
3. Investigate liability and collectibility
4. Demand or pursue claim against wrongdoer
5. Litigate or arbitrate if necessary
6. Recover; reimburse insured deductible and expenses
7. Allocate net recovery (insurer and insured shares)

    Salvage¶

    Type Example
    Salvage property Damaged total-loss vehicle sold or retained
    Salvage of goods Cargo, contents recovered
    Salvage offset Reduce net claim by salvage value
    Proof of salvage Inspection, salvage title, disposition

    Allocation of Recoveries¶

    The allocation of a recovery among insurer, reinsurer, and insured depends on:

    • The excess or primary nature of the policy.
    • The reinsurance treaty terms.
    • The insured's deductible and self-insured retention.
    • Costs and expenses of the recovery.
    Party Recovery Share (typical)
    Insured Deductible recovered first
    Primary insurer Its paid loss
    Reinsurer Its ceded share

    Recovery Ratio and Tracking¶

    Metric Purpose
    Subrogation recovery rate % of paid claims with recoveries
    Salvage recovery rate Salvage vs. paid total losses
    Reinsurance recovery Ceded recoverable received vs. billed
    Net loss ratio Loss ratio after recoveries

    Recovery Controls¶

    Control Purpose
    Recovery pipeline Track open recovery opportunities
    Deadline management Statutes of limitations
    Bordereaux accuracy Correct ceded premiums and claims
    Reconciliations Reinsurer statements vs. records
    Write-off authority Uncollectible recoveries approved

    Summary¶

    • Reinsurance, subrogation, and salvage reduce net claim cost.
    • Reinsurers require timely reporting and documentation.
    • Subrogation requires preserving rights and pursuing wrongdoers.
    • Salvage recovers value from damaged property.
    • Allocation and tracking ensure recoveries are credited properly.
